=== TITLE ===
Quantification of cytokines secreted by primary human cells using multiple reaction monitoring: evaluation of analytical parameters

=== ABSTRACT ===
Determination of secreted proteins provides highly valuable information about cell functions. While the typical methods for the determination of biologically relevant but low abundant molecular species still rely on the use of specific antibodies, mass spectrometry-based methods are now gaining sufficient sensitivity to cope with such challenges as well. In the current study, we have identified several cytokines and chemokines which were induced in primary human umbilical vein endothelial cells upon inflammatory activation. Based on the high-resolution mass spectrometry data obtained with a Q Exactive orbitrap, we built an MRM method to quantify the most relevant molecules selected from the screening experiment. All experimental data are available via ProteomeXchange, PXD002211/12, and Panorama (www.panoramaweb.org). Using nano-flow Chip-HPLC coupled to a 6490 triple-quadrupole MS for MRM analyses, we achieved calibration curves covering a linear range of four orders of magnitude and detection limits in the low attomol per microliter concentration range. Carryover was consistently less than 0.005 %, the accuracy was between 80 and 120 %, and the median coefficient of variation for LC/MS was only 2.2 %. When including the variance of quantification introduced by cell culture and digestion, the coefficient of variation was less than 20 % for most peptides. With appropriate marker molecules, we monitored typical variations introduced by cell culture caused by differences in cell numbers, proliferative states, and cell death. As a result, here, we present a robust and efficient MRM-based assay for the accurate and sensitive determination of cytokines and chemokines representative for functional cell states and including comprehensive quality controls.

=== METHODS ===
Cell culture
Primary human umbilical vein cells (HUVEC) were purchased from Lonza (Walkersville Inc., USA). Cells were cultured in endothelial basal medium supplemented with the EGM-2 SingleQuot Kit (both EBM, Lonza Walkersville Inc., USA), 10 % FCS, and 100 U/mL penicillin/streptomycin (both ATCC, USA), according to the instructions of the manufacturer. Normal human dermal fibroblasts (NHDF) were kindly provided by Verena Paulitschke from the General Hospital of Vienna. Cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 (Life Technologies, UK) supplemented with 10 % FCS and 100 U/mL penicillin/streptomycin (both ATCC, USA). Experiments were performed up to passage 7 and 22 for HUVEC and NHDF, respectively, in 6-well plates using 500,000 cells per well, as determined using a MOXI cell counter (ORFLO, USA). Inflammatory stimulation with 10 ng/mL interleukin-1beta (IL-1β; Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was carried out for 24 h. Cells were then washed once with PBS and cultured in 1.5 mL serum-free medium for 6 h. Subsequently, the supernatants were filtered through 0.2 μm filters (Whatman, Germany) and stored at −80 °C until LC-MS/MS analysis. Viability was routinely assessed by a standard Trypan blue exclusion assay and was consistently better than 98 %. The biological samples were prepared in triplicates to allow statistical analysis.

In-solution digestion
In-solution digestion and subsequent shotgun analysis using a QExactive orbitrap (Thermo) were performed as described recently [5]. For digestion, we used 66 % (1 ml) of the supernatant per sample. Peptide samples were dried in a speed-vac and stored at −20 °C until MS analyses. For shotgun analyses, dried samples were reconstituted in 5 μl 30 % formic acid containing 10 fmol each of four synthetic standard peptides and diluted with 40 μl mobile phase A (98 % H2O, 2 % acetonitrile, 0.1 % formic acid). Synthetic peptides [Glu1-Fribrinopeptide B – EGVNDNEEGFFSAR; M28 – TTPAVLDSDGSYFLYSK; HK0 – VLETKSLYVR; HK1 - VLETK(ε-AC)SLYVR] were obtained from Sigma and Peptide Specialty Laboratories GmbH and spiked in each sample as internal quality control for monitoring LC-MS-system stability. The IL-8-derived peptide VIESGPHCANTEIIVK and the CXCL5-derived peptides EICLDPEAPFLK and CVCLQTTQGVHPK were obtained from Peptide Specialty Laboratories GmbH. For MRM analyses, dried peptide samples were reconstituted in 20 μl 30 % formic acid containing 10 fmol/μl from each of the four synthetic peptides.

Shotgun LC-MS data analysis
Label-free quantitative (LFQ) data analysis was performed using the open source software MaxQuant 1.3.0.5 including the Andromeda search engine [17] and the Perseus statistical analysis package [18, 19] as described previously [5]. Statistical analyses of the identified proteins were performed using Perseus (version 1.3.0.4). Up- and downregulated proteins were determined applying a two-sided t test; the significance threshold indicated in Fig. 3 was calculated using permutation-based multiparameter correction. The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium [20] via the PRIDE partner repository with the dataset identifier PXD002211 and 10.6019/PXD002211 as well as PXD002212 and 10.6019/PXD002212.

MRM assay development
The result files of the MaxQuant search obtained from the shotgun analyses were used to build an MS/MS library in Skyline, version 2.5 [21, 22]. Besides the proteins selected for quality control assessment, as described in the “Results” section, 31 regulated proteins were considered for MRM analysis. Only proteins having a peak area difference of at least fivefold between treated and untreated samples, as determined by shotgun experiments, were selected. From these proteins, prototypic peptides (PTPs) with high Skyline intensity ranks were chosen for analysis (see Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM) Table S1). Peptides containing methionine residues were removed with only one exception in case of CCL2, as, here, no alternative was available. Furthermore, only peptides with zero missed cleavages were considered. Five transitions per peptide were selected from the top ranked y-fragments. As a result, 114 peptides of 31 proteins, comprising 647 transitions in total, were selected. For optimization of collision energies, we used the values predicted by Skyline. Four synthetic non-human peptides were spiked into each sample to support monitoring of the LC-MS runs in case of both modes of analysis, shotgun, and MRM.

The 647 selected transitions were subjected to triple quadrupole mass spectrometry (QQQ-MS), carrying out six consecutive injections of supernatant samples from HUVEC. All peaks were manually inspected using Skyline, and only co-eluting transitions were considered for further validation. The plausibility of the observed retention times was verified via alignment using the four standard peptides together with synthetic peptides from IL-8 and CXCL5. The “dotp” value provided by Skyline, which compares the intensity of MS/MS spectra with MRM fragments, was also considered to align the retention times from the different chromatographic separations. As a result from these evaluation experiments, 115 transitions of 39 peptides derived from 25 proteins were included in the finally scheduled MRM method. All transitions were independently verified using protein samples from other inflammatory stimulated cells, including white blood cells and fibroblasts (data not shown). The minimal dwell time was 15 ms and the cycle time 900 ms. All transitions are listed in ESM Table S2.

LC-MRM analysis
Peptide samples were analyzed by nano-chip-LC using a 1260 Infinity Series HPLC system (Agilent, Palo Alto, CA) coupled to a 6490 triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent). A large capacity protein chip (G4240-62010) with a 160-nl enrichment column and a 150 mm × 75 μm separation column (5 μm ZORBAX 300SB-C18, 30 Å pore size) was used. The injection volume was 1 μl per sample. Mobile phase A consisted of 97.8 % H2O, 2 % acetonitrile, and 0.2 % FA, and mobile phase B consisted of 99.8 % acetonitrile and 0.2 % formic acid. A flow rate of 5 μl/min was applied for sample loading via the cap pump and 400 nl/min for peptide separation via the nano pump. A 20 min gradient (0 min, 0 % B; 1 min, 8 % B; 17 min, 20 % B; 20 min, 30 % B; 21 min, 80 % B; 26 min, 80 % B; 27 min, 40 % B; 28 min, 40 % B; 29 min, 0 % B; 40. 0 % B) was applied for peptide separation. Including column regeneration, the total run time was 40 min. For scheduled MRM, time windows of 5 min were chosen. Electrospray ionization was performed at 1850 V. For MRM mode, at quadrupoles 1 and 2, unit resolution was applied.

MRM data analysis
The calibration curves were obtained using MassHunter Quantitative Analysis B.06 software (Agilent), while the analysis of all other data was performed with Skyline using total peak areas. For the analysis of data comparing untreated and inflammatory-activated HUVEC (Fig. 6), Skyline and Excel were used. Total peak areas of peptides were exported from Skyline via “csv” files, and the additional steps of normalization were performed using Excel. We performed three steps of normalization: firstly, to the average of peak areas of four standard peptides within each MS run; secondly, to the percentage of the sample volume used for digestion; and thirdly, according to the number of cells counted for each sample. Data shown in Fig. 6 were normalized according to protein amounts detected per one million cells. All experimental data associated with this manuscript have been made fully available via the public repository Panorama at www.panoramaweb.org [23] (www.panoramaweb.org/labkey/project/Gerner%20Group%20-%20UVienna/HUVECs%20Cytokinomics_Muqaku%20et%20al.%2C%202015).
