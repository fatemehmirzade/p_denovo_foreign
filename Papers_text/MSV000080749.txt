=== TITLE ===
Development of a targeted selected ion monitoring assay for the elucidation of protease induced structural changes in cardiac troponin T

=== ABSTRACT ===
Cardiac troponin T (cTnT) is a highly cardiospecific protein commonly used in the diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction (AMI), but is subject to proteolytic degradation upon its release in the circulation. In this study, a targeted mass spectrometry assay was developed to detect peptides which are differentially present within the different degradation products. cTnT was spiked in human serum and incubated at 37 °C to induce proteolytic degradation. Isolation and fractionation of cTnT and its fragments from serum were performed using immunoprecipitation and SDS-PAGE. Bands migrating to 37 kDa (intact cTnT), 29 kDa (primary fragment), and 19, 18, and 16 kDa (secondary fragments) were excised, digested, and subsequently analysed using targeted selected ion monitoring on a UHPLC-coupled quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Sixteen precursor ions from a total of 11 peptides unique to cTnT were targeted. Precursor ions were detectable up until 1200 ng/L cTnT, which is a typical cTnT concentration after AMI. With tandem-MS and relative quantification, we proved the formation of cTnT fragments upon incubation in human serum and identified differentially present peptides in the fragment bands, indicative of N- and C-terminal proteolytic cleavage. These findings are of importance for the development of future cTnT assays, calibrators, and quality control samples.

=== METHODS ===
2.1. Reagents and chemicals
Purified human intact cTnT was bought from Hytest (Turku, Finland). Streptavidin coupled M-270 Dynabeads were from Invitrogen (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Protein separation using sodium dodecyl sulphate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) was performed with 12% Criterion Bis–Tris XT Precast Gels, XT MOPS Running Buffer and XT Sample Buffer from Bio-Rad (Hercules, CA, USA). G-250 Bio-Safe™ Coomassie was also from Bio-Rad. Anti-cTnT M11.7 catcher and M7 detector antibodies were a kind gift by Roche Diagnostics (Basel, Switzerland). M7 primary antibody for Western blotting was detected using an HRP-labelled goat anti-mouse polyclonal antibody from Dako (Glostrup, Denmark) and subsequent incubation in Supersignal West Femto chemiluminescent substrate from Thermo Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA).
Thirteen cTnT peptides of interest were synthesised by Pepscan (Lelystad, the Netherlands) with an average purity > 95%. Numbering of peptide sequences throughout this manuscript is based on the canonical human protein species cTnT (cTnT-1, P45379). Peptide sequences with a lowercase, bold type, m represent peptides with an oxidised methionine.
Generic reagents and chemicals used for the digestion of proteins were UPLC-grade acetonitrile (ACN) and water from Biosolve (Valkenswaard, the Netherlands), ≥ 99.5% ammonium bicarbonate from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA) and trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Proteins in excised bands were reduced and alkylated using dithiothreitol (DTT) and iodoacetamide (IAA) from GE Healthcare (Little Chalfont, United Kingdom). In-gel digestion of the protein bands was performed with mass spectrometry grade Trypsin Gold in the presence of ProteaseMAX™ surfactant (both from Promega, Madison, WI, USA).
Additional reagents used during sample work-up prior to digestion were of reagent grade.
2.2. Serum samples
In this study, the majority of experiments were performed using a pool of cTnT-negative (< 14 ng/L) serum of healthy volunteers participating in a health-check programme organised yearly at our hospital. This cTnT-negative human serum pool was used to prepare the dilution, incubation, and technical replicate series used for the validation of our assay.
A Western blot was created using 5 leftover serum samples from a patient routinely measured at our laboratory. The patient was diagnosed with ST-segment elevated myocardial infarction (STEMI). All 5 samples were diluted using the cTnT-negative human serum pool to a similar cTnT concentration corresponding to 2 ng cTnT loaded on the gel.
The specificity of the assay was assessed with the cTnT-negative human serum pool and with leftover serum samples from 2 patients routinely measured at our laboratory (cTnT concentration < 14 ng/L). One patient was diagnosed with multiple myeloma and had a total protein content of 95.5 g/L. The other patient was diagnosed with gallstone disease. Both serum samples were selected because of their complex background proteome and negative cTnT values.
Handling of all serum samples was in accordance with the code for proper secondary use of human tissue in the Netherlands (www.fmwv.nl).
2.3. Sample preparation
For validation of the method, purified human intact cTnT was spiked in the cTnT-negative human serum pool at an initial concentration of 150 μg/L and was subsequently serially diluted using the same cTnT-negative serum. One of those samples was used as a technical replicate and consisted of cTnT spiked in the human serum pool at a concentration of 100 μg/L. This sample was split in 6 different replicates which were all separately processed and analysed. For application of the method, purified human intact cTnT was spiked in cTnT-negative pooled serum at a concentration of 1.5 mg/L and incubated at 37 °C for 0, 4, 8, 24, 32, and 48 h. This experiment has been performed three times independently. Three cTnT-negative serum samples used for assessing the specificity of the assay remained unspiked and received the same treatment as above-mentioned samples. All samples were then stored at − 80 °C upon further sample processing.
2.4. cTnT concentration measurements
Just prior to the start of the workflow, measurements of the cTnT concentration in each sample were performed with the 5th generation (high-sensitive) cTnT immunoassay on the Cobas® 6000 instrument (Roche Diagnostics) with a limit of blank (LOB) of 3 ng/L, a limit of detection (LOD) of 5 ng/L, a 10% CV of 13 ng/L and a linear measuring range of 3.00–10,000 ng/L, according to the product insert. Concentrations were expressed in ng/L equivalent intact cTnT.
2.5. Immunoprecipitation
Immunoprecipitation of serum samples was performed based upon the method by Michielsen et al. [27]. Briefly, 10 μg biotinylated M11.7 antibody (epitope specificity: 146EQQRIRNEREKE157) was cross-linked to 1 mL M-270 Streptavidin dynabeads. Fifty microlitres of beads was then incubated with 200 μL serum for 1 h at 4 °C. After washing the beads 4 times in PBS with 0.1% (v/v) Tween-20, immunoprecipitated cTnT was eluted in 25 μL 0.1 M citrate at pH 2.5 for 15 min at 56 °C. Seven microlitres of XT Sample Buffer was added to the eluates. One microlitre of the resulting sample was diluted 100 times for Western blotting; the remaining sample was used for SDS-PAGE and Coomassie staining.
2.6. SDS-PAGE and Western blotting
After stacking for 15 min at 100 V, proteins were separated on 12% criterion XT precast gels for 45 min at 200 V using XT MOPS as running buffer.
Gels intended for Western blotting were then blotted on 0.45 μm nitrocellulose membranes (Bio-Rad) for 1 h at 100 V. Application of antibodies on the nitrocellulose membrane was performed using the SNAP i.d.® 2.0 protein detection system from Millipore (Billerica, MA, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The primary antibody used was the monoclonal M7 detector antibody by Roche Diagnostics (epitope specificity: 135DRIERR140). The secondary antibody was HRP-labelled goat anti mouse polyclonal antibody (GAMPO). Blots with attached antibodies were incubated in 1 mL Supersignal West Femto for 3 min before being detected with a Chemidoc XRS + system (Bio-Rad).
Gels intended for Coomassie staining were first fixed in 100 mL 45% methanol, 10% acetic acid solution for 1 h, followed by staining with Bio-Safe™ Coomassie for 1 h under gentle agitation. Stained gels were washed overnight in water to reduce background after which an image was captured with a GS-800 calibrated densitometer (Bio-Rad). Gels were subsequently stored in a 1% acetic acid solution (for a maximum of 1 week at 4 °C) until digestion.
2.7. In-gel digestion
Coomassie stained gel bands were carefully excised using a sharp razor blade in a flow cabinet. Gel slices were washed with 200 μL water. Precipitated proteins in the gel slices were reduced with 6.5 mM DTT for 1 h at room temperature, followed by alkylation in 54 mM IAA for 30 min at room temperature in the dark. The proteins were then digested with 60 ng trypsin in the presence of 0.01% ProteaseMAX™ surfactant for 3 h at 37 °C. The surfactant facilitates protein digestion by solubilising proteins and providing a denaturing environment. This eliminates the need for separate peptide extraction steps and drastically lowers digestion time [28]. All reagents (DTT, IAA, ProteaseMax, and trypsin) were dissolved in 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate, pH 7.8. In-between each step, the gel slices were dehydrated twice with 100 μL ACN before rehydration. TFA was added to the extracted peptides to a final concentration of 0.5% before LC–MS/MS analysis.
2.8. Mass spectrometry
Peptides of interest were selected by the in silico digestion of cTnT using the targeted proteomics software platform Skyline [29]. A synthetic standard was created by pooling 13 synthesised cTnT peptides of interest in equimolar amounts. This standard was used to determine the retention time, ideal charge state and optimal normalised collision energy (NCE) of each individual peptide, as described in [30]. In addition, this standard was used as a quality control throughout the study and used to schedule the targeted measurements using an RT window of either 2 or 4 min.
LC–MS/MS measurements were performed on a Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer, connected to a UHPLC Dionex Ultimate 3000 by Thermo Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). Solvent A consisted of 0.1% formic acid (FA) in 100% water, solvent B consisted of 0.08% FA, 20% water, and 80% ACN. Peptides were first trapped on an Acclaim PepMap 100, 100 μm × 2 cm, C18, 5 μm, 100 A trap column in 0.1% TFA, 2% ACN and 98% water. Peptides were subsequently separated on an Acclaim PepMap RSLC, 75 μm × 15 cm, C18, 2 μm, 100 A analytical column by a 30 min gradient of 4–55% buffer B, followed by 55–90% B in 1 min and 90% B for 4 min at a flow rate of 300 nL/min. SIM was performed on selected m/z values as depicted in Table 1 at a resolution of 70,000 FWHM at m/z 200, automatic gain control (AGC) of 100,000, maximum injection time of 250 ms and a detection window of 2.0 Th. Data dependent MS/MS was performed using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) and detection of fragment spectra with an isolation window of 0.6 Th at a resolution of 35,000 FWHM at m/z 200, AGC of 50,000 and a maximum injection time of 120 ms. NCE was optimised for each peptide and is shown in Table 1. Dynamic exclusion was 10 s.

To minimise the amount of carryover, samples from the dilution series were analysed from low to high cTnT concentration. Samples from the incubation series were analysed from low to high molecular mass. In addition, blank injections (98% water, 2% ACN, and 0.05% TFA) were performed in-between each single sample. The amount of carryover was determined by measuring 2 blank injections after the measurement of a sample and determining the percentage intensity of the second blank injection compared to the sample intensity. This measurement was performed in duplicate.
2.9. Database search and data analysis
Measured MS/MS spectra were analysed using Proteome Discoverer, version 1.8 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The database used was the reviewed human UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot database (dated November 13, 2013) including all reviewed protein isoforms. The sequences of the common Repository of Adventitious Proteins (cRAP) were added for a total of 39,690 entries. Product ion spectra were searched against this database using the search engine SEQUEST. Precursor and product ion mass tolerances were set to 10 ppm and 0.1 Da, respectively. Enzyme specificity was set to semi-tryptic with 2 missed cleavages. Fixed value PSM validator was used at default Xcorr cut-offs. All accepted spectra were manually evaluated.
Precursor ion chromatograms were analysed using Skyline version 2.6 [29]. The area under the curve (AUC) of each monoisotopic precursor (M) and its M + 1 and M + 2 isotopologues was calculated and summed; obtaining the total AUC of the corresponding precursor ion. For a chromatographic peak to be accepted, it needs to conform to the following three criteria: first, its retention time needs to be within ± 0.2 min of that of the synthetic standard. Second, its precursor ion mass error is at most ± 5 ppm. And third, a minimal isotopic dot product (idotp) of 0.95, and/or an adequate MS/MS spectrum positively identifying the selected peak. Dilution curves were created by plotting the abundance of each precursor ion as a function of the measured cTnT concentration of serially diluted samples in serum. The limit of detection (LOD) is defined as the lowest cTnT concentration from the dilution series which was identified according to the above criteria.
For the application of the method, a so-called precursor/reference (P/R) ratio was calculated between the total AUC of each selected precursor ion (P) and the sum of the total AUCs of all selected precursor ions of a reference peptide (R). The reference peptide chosen was the peptide 187ALSNMMHFGGYIQK200. Because it contains 2 methionine residues, 4 precursor ions were selected corresponding to its 4 most common oxidation states (two single-oxidised, a double-oxidised and a non-oxidised state). Other methionine-containing peptides with multiple precursor ions also had their respective total AUCs summed to correct for differences in oxidation between samples. Each calculated P/R ratio was normalised with respect to the highest value within the independent incubation experiments. Three such experiments were performed; each of which consisted of the measurement of 5 cTnT bands from 4 serum samples (so a total of 20 MS measurements per independent experiment). The normalised P/R ratios of all bands with the same molecular weight where then presented as mean ± SEM with a sample size of 12, based on 3 independent experiments. Comparison of different ratios was performed using a one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Bonferroni analysis where p ≤ 0.01 was considered statistically significant.
