=== TITLE ===
Validation of a Novel Shotgun Proteomic Workflow for the Discovery of Protein–Protein Interactions: Focus on ZNF521

=== ABSTRACT ===
The study of protein–protein interactions is increasingly relying on mass spectrometry (MS). The classical approach of separating immunoprecipitated proteins by SDS-PAGE followed by in-gel digestion is long and labor-intensive. Besides, it is difficult to integrate it with most quantitative MS-based workflows, except for stable isotopic labeling of amino acids in cell culture (SILAC). This work describes a fast, flexible and quantitative workflow for the discovery of novel protein–protein interactions. A cleavable cross-linker, dithiobis[succinimidyl propionate] (DSP), is utilized to stabilize protein complexes before immunoprecipitation. Protein complex detachment from the antibody is achieved by limited proteolysis. Finally, protein quantitation is performed via 18O labeling. The workflow has been optimized concerning (i) DSP concentration and (ii) incubation times for limited proteolysis, using the stem cell-associated transcription cofactor ZNF521 as a model target. The interaction of ZNF521 with the core components of the nuclear remodelling and histone deacetylase (NuRD) complex, already reported in the literature, was confirmed. Additionally, interactions with newly discovered molecular partners of potentially relevant functional role, such as ZNF423, Spt16, Spt5, were discovered and validated by Western blotting.

=== METHODS ===
Cell Lines and Culture Conditions

HEK293T and DAOY cells were cultured in DMEM and the hematopoietic cell line K562 in RPM1 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 50 U of penicillin and 50 μg of streptomycin/mL, at 37 °C in 5% CO2. All tissue culture reagents were from Life Technologies.
Expression Vectors

The ZNF521 expression plasmids FUIGW-ZNF521 and FUIGW-ZNF521 ΔNBD (lacking the first 13 amino acids that constitute the NuRD-binding motif) have been previously described. (29-31) Plasmids p3xFlag-CMV-7.1 expressing the first 1–15, 1–20 zinc fingers and the last 24–30 zinc fingers for ZNF521 were described in Bond et al. (32) and Mega et al. (33) The Zfp423 Myc-DDK-tagged clone in pCMV6 entry (MR221944, NM_033327) was from OriGene. The plasmid for ZNF423 (pFlag-CS2-OAZ, NM-001271620) and pFlag-CS2-OAZ-ZNF-1–13 were as used by Hata et al. (34) and lack the N-terminal NuRD binding motif. The ZNF521 was cloned in-frame into the pEGFP-C3 vector (BD Biosciences) for the GFP-ZNF521 fusion protein.
Transfection

Plasmids were transfected in using calcium phosphate method in HEK293T cell using 10 μg/100 mm tissue culture plate. Medium was changed after 16 h and cells harvested for immunoprecipitation after 48 h. K562 and DAOY cells were transduced with the control vector FUIGW, FUIGW-ZNF521 or FUIGW-ZNF521 ΔNBD using the lentiviral packaging plasmids pCMV-VSVG and pCMV-deltaR8–91 as described in Spina et al. (30) Transduced cells were 70–80% positive for the transgene EGFP by facs analysis and stably expressed the Flag-ZNF521 and Flag-ZNF521 ΔNBD at similar high levels.
DSP Cross-Linking

To stabilize weak and transient interactions between ZNF521 and its binding partners, cells were chemically cross-linked with dithiobis (succinimidyl propionate) DSP reagent from Thermo Fisher Scientific (# 22585). DSP was prepared as initially in DMSO at 10 mg/mL and diluted into PBS to final concentrations of 0.05, 0.25 and 1 mM at 37 °C before adding to the cells and incubating for 2 h on ice. The reaction was stopped with the addition of 20 mM TrisHCl pH 7.4 for 15 min on ice.
Cell Lysis and Coimmunoprecipitation

Cells were cultured to 60–70% confluence and were transfected as described above after 48 h cells were cross-linked, washed twice with PBS and pelleted at 1000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C to be then resuspended in immunoprecipitation (IP) buffer (50 mM sodium phosphate pH 7.2, 250 mM NaCl, 0.1% Triton X-100, 0.1 nM ZnCl2) supplemented with protease (for Histidine tagged proteins P8849 Sigma) and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails 2 and 3 (P0044, P5726 Sigma). Cell lysates were sonicated three times for 10 s and were centrifuged at 13 000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C to remove debris. Their total protein concentrations were measured with the Bio-Rad protein assay.
Two different strategies to immunoprecipitate interactors of ZNF521 were used: (a) immunoprecipitation with a purified murine IgG1 monoclonal antibody covalently attached to agarose (anti-Flag M2 agarose Affinity Gel, 1.7–2.1 g antibody/liter gel, Sigma-Aldrich); or (b) immunoprecipitation with 10 μg of purified IgG1 monoclonal antibody (Monoclonal ANTI-Flag M2, Clone M2, Sigma-Aldrich) to be then bound by immobilized Protein G (Protein G Sepharose 4B fast flow, capacity of binding >20 mg IgG/mL of resin P3296, Sigma-Aldrich). Cell lysates were added to agarose beads and incubated overnight at 4 °C with rotation. After several washes with IP buffer and PBS, to elute ZNF521 and its binding partners two different approaches were used: the classical one, in which the bound proteins were released by heating immunoprecipitates at 95 °C for 5 min in NuPAGE LDS Sample Buffer plus reducing agent and loaded on a NuPAGE Novex Bis-Tris 4–12% gel (Life Techologies). Alternatively, ZNF521 and its interactors were released by trypsin from the resin as described in the section below (On-Bead Digestion).
Western Blotting

Proteins were electroblotted onto nitrocellulose membranes and detected with Ponceau S staining before blocking with 5% skim milk powder (70166, Fluka) in PBS, 0.25% Tween 20. Primary antibodies used for the detection of immunoprecipitated proteins were anti-FLAG-M2-HRP (A8592), GFP (G1544), MTA2 (M7569), HDAC1 (H3284), RBBP7 (SAB3104734) and p66alpha GATAD2A (HPA006759) used at 1:10 000 and were from Sigma-Aldrich. Antibodies for HDAC2 (sc6296) and RBBP4, RbAp48 (sc12434), MBD3 (sc9402), Spt5 (sc28678) and Spt16 (sc28734) used at 1:2000 and were from Santa Cruz Biotechnologies. Secondary antibodies, antirabbit IgG-HRP (sc2054), antimouse IgG-HRP (sc2055) and antigoat IgG-HRP (sc2056) were detected by the ImmunoCruz Western blotting luminal reagent (Santa Cruz Biotechnology) and exposure to autoradiographic film.
In-Gel Digestion

Gel was washed twice with water to remove excess of running buffer and then stained overnight with EZ Blue Gel Staining Reagent, Sigma-Aldrich. After destaining, bands were excised from the gel under a laminar flow hood and subjected to in-gel digestion. (35) Briefly, excised bands were cut into small cubes and transferred into clean microcentrifuge tubes to be destained with ammonium bicarbonate/acetonitrile 1:1 for 30 min at room temperature (RT), reduced by incubating with 10 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) for 1 h at 37 °C, alkylated by treatment with iodoacetamide (IAA) 55 mM for 20 min at RT in the dark and finally digested with trypsin for 30 min on ice and then, overnight, at 37 °C with agitation. Residual peptides were released by incubating bands with 5% formic acid/acetonitrile 1:2 for 15 min at 37 °C. The resulting tryptic peptides were dried by speed vacuum, resuspended in TFA 0,1% and desalted on StageTip C18 reverse phase resin. (36)
On-Bead Digestion

ZNF521 and its interactors were released from the resin by digestion for either 5, 15, or 30 min with trypsin (Proteomic grade Sigma) at 37 °C with shaking (500 rpm) in 100 μL digestion buffer (100 mM Tris/HCl pH 8.5, 0.1% Triton X-100, 400 ng trypsin) for each sample. After on-bead digestion by trypsin, the supernatant was collected and subjected to conventional in-solution tryptic digestion. Briefly, proteins were reduced by 10 mM DTT for 1h at 37 °C with agitation. This treatment reverses peptide DSP cross-linking as well. Cysteines were alkylated by treatment with IAA, final concentration 24 mM, for 1 h at 37 °C with agitation in the dark; the alkylation reaction was quenched by adding 2 mM DTT (final concentration) for 30 min at 37 °C. Finally, proteins were incubated with 200 ng trypsin overnight at 37 °C with agitation to complete digestion. The resulting tryptic peptides were dried by speed vacuum, resuspended in TFA 0.1% and desalted on StageTip C18 reverse phase resin. Before nanoLC–MS/MS analysis, peptide solutions were evaporated to dryness and either resuspended in 40 μL of mobile phase A for direct injection or fractionated using SCX StageTips (see below).
Discovery of ZNF521 Interactors: 18O Labeling

Before labeling, tryptic peptides were dried using speed vacuum; dried peptides from control sample (CTR, immunoprecipitates from cells transfected with the empty vector) were labeled with 16O and peptides from ZNF521 (full length) and ZNF521-ΔNBD and their binding partners were labeled with 18O. For labeling, peptides were resuspended in 50 μL of 80% water (H216O for CTR, H218O for ZNF521 and ZNF521-ΔNBD IPs), 20% methanol and incubated overnight at 37 °C in the presence of 200 ng of trypsin to catalyze oxygen exchange. To inactivate trypsin, samples were heated for 1 h at 56 °C and boiled for 10 min at 100 °C. After verifying labeling efficiency through LC–MS/MS analysis, pairs of differently labeled samples were mixed in 1:1 ratio (ZNF521 vs CTL and ZNF521-ΔNBD vs CTL). Experiments were performed in duplicate.
As mentioned before, labeling efficiency was assessed by injecting 2% of the digest of both full-length ZNF521 immunoprecipitates after 18O labeling and trypsin inactivation.18O incorporation was evident in over 96% of detected peptides (Supporting Information 1a and 1b).
The procedure of trypsin inactivation, first reported by Petritis et al., achieves efficient minimization of oxygen back-exchange, allowing heavy labeled peptides to be stable for at least 1 week at room temperature before injection into a nanoLC–MS/MS system. (37)
nanoLC–MS/MS

Chromatography was performed on an Easy LC 1000 nanoscale liquid chromatography (nanoLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Odense, Denmark). The analytical nanoLC column was a pulled fused silica capillary, 75 μm i.d., in-house packed to a length of 10 cm with 3 μm C18 silica particles from Dr. Maisch (Entringen, Germany). Four microliters of the peptide mixtures were loaded at 500 nL/min directly onto the analytical column. A binary gradient was used for peptide elution. Mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid, 2% acetonitrile, whereas mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid, 80% acetonitrile. Gradient elution was achieved at a 300 nL/min flow rate, and ramped from 2% B to 35% B in 70 min, and from 35% B to 100% B in additional 5 min; after 5 min at 100% B, the column was re-equilibrated to 2% B for 10 min before the following injection. MS detection was performed on a quadrupole-orbitrap mass spectrometer Q-Exactive (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) operating in positive ion mode, with nanoelectrospray (nESI) potential at 1800 V applied on the column front-end via a tee piece. Data-dependent acquisition was performed by using a top-12 method, where the 12 most abundant ions were automatically selected for HCD fragmentation at normalized collision energy of 25%. Resolution (fwhm), AGC target and maximum injection time (ms) for full MS and MS/MS were 70 000/17 500, (1 × 106)/(1 × 105), 20/60, respectively. MS full scan range was 350–1800 m/z. Mass window for precursor ion isolation was 1.6 m/z. Ion threshold for triggering MS/MS events was 2 × 104. Dynamic exclusion was 30 s.
For the quantitative analysis of novel ZNF521 interactors, an additional SCX fractionation step was performed using strong cation exchange (SCX) StageTips. (36) Briefly, the SCX StageTip fractionation was performed in tip columns prepared from 200 μL micropipette tips stacked with one layer of a strong cation exchange resin. Before purification, each sample was 20-fold diluted in 80% acetonitrile +0.5% formic acid (solution SCX-a) to dilute the tris buffer originally present in the sample to a final concentration of 5 mM. The diluted peptide solution was loaded onto the StageTip after tip conditioning in solution SCX-a. After a washing step with solution SCX-a, stepwise elution of tryptic peptides was achieved by sequential addition of 7 μL of four solutions, all containing 20% acetonitrile, of increasing ionic strength and pH: (i) 75 mM ammonium acetate, 0.5% formic acid; (ii) 150 mM ammonium acetate, 0.5% formic acid; (iii) 250 mM ammonium acetate, 0.5% formic acid; (iv) 500 mM ammonium acetate. The four SCX fractions were evaporated by vacuum centrifugation and resuspended in 8 μL of mobile phase A for nanoLC–MS/MS analysis, accomplished as described above.
Data Analysis

Data were processed using Proteome Discoverer 1.3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany), using Sequest as search engine, and the HUMAN Uniprot protein sequence database (http://www.ebi.ac.uk/uniprot) accessed on March 2013 as sequence database (with isoforms, 87 489 entries). The following search parameters were used: MS tolerance 15 ppm; MS/MS tolerance 0.02 Da; fixed modifications carbamidomethyl cysteine; enzyme trypsin; max. missed cleavages 1; variable modifications: oxidized methionine, C-terminal 18O labeling. The variable modification CAM-thiopropanoyl (K) was not employed as search parameter, because it did not provide, for this particular data set, significant improvements with respect to the number of protein identifications. Peptide identifications were validated by Percolator, (38) integrated in Proteome Discoverer. Percolator q-value was set to equal or less than 0.05. Nevertheless, only proteins identified with at least one peptide having a q-value equal or less than 0.01 were considered for further analysis (but for quantification, peptides with a q-value between 0.01 and 0.05 were also utilized).
For label-free experiments (optimization of DSP concentration and optimization of on-bead digestion), quantification at the peptide level was achieved within Proteome Discoverer by using the event detector (mass precision 2 ppm) and precursor ion area detector modules to calculate peptide peak areas. The sum of all peptide peak areas assigned to ZNF521 or any other protein was used as a semiquantitative measure of protein abundance. For the final experiment (discovery of novel ZNF521 interactors), based on 18O labeling, quantification was achieved within Proteome Discoverer by using the event detector (mass precision 2 ppm) and precursor ion area quantifier (full 18O labeling) modules to calculate peptide “heavy:light” (H:L) ratios. Protein H:L ratios were calculated by taking the median value of all peptide H:L ratios assigned to a specific protein hit. Average protein H:L ratios obtained from replicate experiments were calculated in log space and then transformed back into linear space. For constructing protein interaction networks, the STRING database (version 9.3) was used. (39)
The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium (http://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org) via the PRIDE partner repository (40) with the data set identifier PXD001609 and DOI: 10.6019/PXD001609. The data was converted using PRIDE Converter (http://pride-converter.googlecode.com).
