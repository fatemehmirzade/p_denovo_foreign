=== TITLE ===
EBprot: Statistical analysis of labeling-based quantitative proteomics data

=== ABSTRACT ===
Labeling-based proteomics is a powerful method for detection of differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). The current data analysis platform typically relies on protein-level ratios, which is obtained by summarizing peptide-level ratios for each protein. In shotgun proteomics, however, some proteins are quantified with more peptides than others, and this reproducibility information is not incorporated into the differential expression (DE) analysis. Here, we propose a novel probabilistic framework EBprot that directly models the peptide-protein hierarchy and rewards the proteins with reproducible evidence of DE over multiple peptides. To evaluate its performance with known DE states, we conducted a simulation study to show that the peptide-level analysis of EBprot provides better receiver-operating characteristic and more accurate estimation of the false discovery rates than the methods based on protein-level ratios. We also demonstrate superior classification performance of peptide-level EBprot analysis in a spike-in dataset. To illustrate the wide applicability of EBprot in different experimental designs, we applied EBprot to a dataset for lung cancer subtype analysis with biological replicates and another dataset for time course phosphoproteome analysis of EGF-stimulated HeLa cells with multiplexed labeling. Through these examples, we show that the peptide-level analysis of EBprot is a robust alternative to the existing statistical methods for the DE analysis of labeling-based quantitative datasets. The software suite is freely available on the Sourceforge website http://ebprot.sourceforge.net/. All MS data have been deposited in the ProteomeXchange with identifier PXD001426 (http://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org/dataset/PXD001426/).

2.1 Outlier detection

Before we estimate the model parameters and score proteins for DE, we first apply an outlier removal procedure to the peptide-ratio data. The purpose of outlier detection is to identify peptides with aberrant ratios away from the median ratio (in log scale) in each protein, where the degree of ratio variability must be within a certain tolerable bound. To do this, we select all the proteins with at least m peptides (default urn:x-wiley:16159853:media:pmic12008:pmic12008-math-0001) and estimate the standard deviation of peptide log ratios for each protein. Next, we take the median SD as the “reference” SD, denoted by ω, and go through individual proteins with at least two peptides and remove a peptide if it is outside urn:x-wiley:16159853:media:pmic12008:pmic12008-math-0002 range around the median log ratio of the corresponding protein. The choice of the factor 4 is to ensure that only the extreme outliers are removed since the interval covers >99.9% of the range if the data are generated under Gaussian distribution. Should all peptides fail to meet this criteria (e.g. proteins with two peptides), the protein will be removed from the analysis and the list of removed proteins is reported in the software.

2.3 Mode of analysis in various experimental designs

The aforementioned model was built for comparison of two samples or conditions. Nevertheless the modeling framework can be easily extended to analyze data in multigroup comparisons or replicate designs as long as there is no systematic difference between different group comparisons and the ratios can therefore be merged into a single dataset. Here, we describe how EBprot handles such situations and what it reports in three different scenarios.

2.3.1 Independent experiments

The first scenario is when the dataset has multiple labeling experiments and each experiment corresponds to the comparison of independent biological samples. In this case, we recommend that EBprot be applied to each ratio separately through the “Independent Comparison” option, which performs analysis as such in a single command line call. This implies that the hierarchical mixture model will be fitted and the PPscore and BFDR will also be derived separately for each ratio dataset.

2.3.2 Replicate experiments

The second scenario is when labeling experiments are performed with technical and/or biological replicates for the same comparison. “Technical replicate” refers to a repeated MS experiment of the same biological material (without labeling the samples again), whereas “Biological replicate” refers to a repeated MS experiment of newly labeled biological samples (e.g. forward and reverse labeling of the same biological samples). Ideally one would expect a single confidence score for the same protein since the set of experiments gives repeated measurements across replicate experiments. However, we have observed that these replicate MS runs can introduce systematic shifts in the ratio distributions and merging the ratio datasets for joint modeling can introduce artifacts. For this reason, with the “ReplicateDesign” option, we suggest that the ratios from technical replicates be averaged prior to the analysis (discarding peptides that do not appear in all technical replicates if desired), and the ratios from biological replicates be used as the input. Under this design option, EBprot fits replicate-specific models and provides a post hoc processing step to indicate in how many replicates each protein was up- and downregulated at a given BFDR threshold.

2.3.3 Multiplexed experiments

In the third scenario, multiple samples are compared in a single labeling experiment through multiplexed labeling (e.g. multiplexed iTRAQ and TMT labeling). These datasets can be analyzed by the “MultiplexDesign” option. Here, EBprot treats the ratios for different pairs of conditions for the same protein as different ratios, but models them in one integrated mixture model and computes the probability scores and associated BFDR simultaneously. This design option is thus suitable for time course experiments or the analysis of the same biological sample across multiple conditions (e.g. time course following a treatment), assuming that the reference channel of the labeling was common and all labeled samples were analyzed in a single MS experiment.

2.4 Peptide-protein mapping

When peptide-level quantitative data are rolled up to protein-level statistical summary, an important issue is the handling of shared peptides. In our current implementation of EBprot, we assume that the peptides with unique mapping to a protein or a protein group are used for quantification. In this work, we used peptide-level MaxQuant output data (UPS1) or published data with preassigned protein IDs (lung cancer and EGFR-stimulated HeLa cell data), and thus the protein–peptide hierarchy information was already fixed. In the UPS1 and lung cancer datasets, we took the leading Uniprot IDs reported in the “Proteins” column as protein IDs and computed the scores for those proteins. For the phosphoproteome data, since we analyzed the dataset at the peptide level with unique site localization following the original analysis, we did not suffer from the issue of handling shared peptides.

2.5.2 UPS1 benchmark SILAC dataset

HCT116 cells were SILAC-labeled by culturing them in SILAC DMEM (Invitrogen) containing either 13C- and 15N-labeled lysine and arginine (K8R10; heavy) (Cambridge Isotopes) or 12C- and 14N-labeled lysine and arginine (K0R0; light) (Sigma-Aldrich), 10% dialyzed FBS (Thermo Scientific), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco) at 37° C in a humidity-saturated 5% CO2 atmosphere. The cells were cultured for approximately six doublings to ensure complete incorporation before harvesting. Cells were washed once with PBS before lysis using 8 M urea in 100 mM ammonium bicarbonate. Protein concentration was determined using the Bradford assay (Bio-rad).

The lyophilized UPS1 (Sigma-Aldrich) was reconstituted in a buffer containing 8 M urea and 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate. A series of 100, 300, and 600 fmol of UPS1 proteins were spiked into a constant cell lysate mix containing 2 μg of light- and 2μg of heavy-labeled HCT116 Parental. Reduction, followed by alkylation, was carried out on the samples with 5 mM DTT (Sigma-Aldrich) and 10 mM iodoacetamide (Sigma-Aldrich), respectively, at room temperature for 30 min. In-solution digestion was performed using Lys-C (Promega) at 37° C for 16 h, followed by trypsin (Promega) at 37° C for 8 h. Desalting was carried out on self-packed C18 stage-tips. The eluted peptides were separated by reverse-phase chromatography on Thermo Scientific Easy-Spray column (PepMap® RSLC, C18, 2mm, 100Å, 75 mm × 50 cm) using Thermo Scientific nanoflow HPLC system (Easy-nLC 1000). Easy-nLC 1000 was coupled to Thermo Scientific Q Exactive benchtop Quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Peptides were loaded into column with buffer A (0.1% formic acid, 2% acetonitrile) and eluted with a 190 min gradient at a flow rate of 200 nL/min from 8 to 40% buffer B (0.1% formic acid, 80% acetonitrile) for 180 min and till 100% buffer for 10 min.

Mass spectra were acquired in the positive ion mode with MS2. Full MS used a resolution of 70 000 with target value of 3E6 for 10 ms ranging from 310 to 2000 m/z. MS2 resolution was 17 500 with target value of 5E4 for 50 ms using top 20 most intense peaks with isolation window of 2 m/z. NCE of 25 and dynamic exclusion window of 15 s were used. The ion intensity threshold was 1E4. All precursor and fragment mass tolerances were set to 20 ppm/0.5 Da in the Q Exactive benchtop Quadrupole-Orbitrap.

The UPS1 datasets were searched against the human sequence database from UniProt Knowledgebase (2014) with a total of 88 479 sequences. Database searches were performed with tryptic specificity allowing maximum two missed cleavages and labeled arginine and lysine. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine was specified as a fixed modification with N-acetylation and oxidized methionine permitted as a variable modification. SILAC peptide and protein quantification was performed with MaxQuant version 1.3.0.5. The proteins were considered identified when supported by at least one unique peptide with a minimum length of seven amino acids. Maximum FDR was set to 0.01 for both protein and peptide.

The MS proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium 20 via the PRIDE partner repository with the project accession identifier PXD001426 (username: reviewer45653@ebi.ac.uk, password 9ClmKEik).

2.5.4 Perseus

For Perseus analysis, we used the default options for SigB score calculation. The significance B module in Perseus calculates p-values for the detection of significant ratios, applied to subsets of proteins grouped by an intensity binning procedure. The p-values are computed as the tail probabilities in the Gaussian distribution of the log-ratios in each subset. We used the protein level ratios and intensities reported in the MaxQuant protein-level output file (ProteinGroups.txt) as the input in Perseus.
