=== TITLE ===
Modulating the selectivity of affinity absorbents to multi-phosphopeptides by a competitive substitution strategy

=== ABSTRACT ===
Although many affinity adsorbents have been developed for phosphopeptides enrichment, high-specifically capturing the multi-phosphopeptides is still a big challenge. Here, we investigated the mechanism of phosphate ion coordination and substitution on affinity adsorbents surfaces and modulated the selectivity of affinity adsorbents to multi-phosphopeptides based on the different capability of mono- and multi-phosphopeptides in competitively substituting the pre-coordinated phosphate ions at strong acidic condition. We demonstrated both the species of pre-coordinated phosphate ions and the substituting conditions played crucial roles in modulating the enrichment selectivity to multi-phosphopeptides, and the pre-coordinated affinity materials with relative more surfaces positive charges exhibited better enrichment efficiency due to the cooperative effect of electrostatic interaction and competitive substitution. Finally, an enrichment selectivity of 85% to multi-phosphopeptides was feasibly achieved with 66% improvement in identification numbers for complex protein sample extracted from HepG2 cells. Data are available via ProteomeXchange with identifier PXD004252.

=== METHODS ===
2.1. Protein samples preparation
α-Casein (1 mg/mL) was dissolved into 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl (pH 8.0). The trypsin was added with a mass ratio of 1:25 (enzyme: protein, w/w) and the digestion was performed overnight at 37 °C. The digestion was quenched by trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) acidification.
HepG2 cells were cultured in RPIM-1640 with 10% bovine serum at 37 °C in 5% CO2 and harvested at about 90% of the petri dish. The cells pellets were homogenized in an ice-cold lysis buffer containing 8 mol/L urea, 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4), 65 mmol/L dithiothreitol (DTT), 2% protease cocktail (v/v), 1% Triton X-100 (v/v), 1 mmol/L (ethylenedinitrilo)tetraacetic acid disodium salt (EDTA), 1 mmol/L ethylene glycol-bis(2-aminoethylether)-tetraacetic acid (EGTA), 1 mmol/L NaF, 1 mmol/L Na3VO4, 1 mmol/L Sodium β-glycerophosphate and 5 mmol/L Na4O7P2 assisted by an intense sonication (400 W × 60 s × 3). The cell debris was removed by centrifugation at 25000g for 30 min the supernatant was treated with 5 vol of ice cold acetone and kept at −30 °C overnight. The protein pellet was obtained by centrifugation at 25000g for 30 min. After re-dissolvation into 8 M urea and 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) buffer, the protein concentration was determined by BCA assay. The protein sample was reduced and alkylated by DTT and iodoacetamide (IAA). Then the concentration of urea was diluted to 1 mol/L with 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), followed with overnight digestion by adding trypsin with a ratio of 1:25 (enzyme: protein, w/w).
2.2. Enrichment of multi-phosphopeptides by phosphate ion pre-coordinated affinity materials
The Ti4+-IMAC adsorbents were home-made according to the work of Zhou [16]. 1 mg Ti4+-IMAC adsorbents were firstly incubated with 200 μL 10 mmol/L phosphate salt solution (orthophosphate, β-glycerophosphate or pyrophosphate) for 30 min and stepwise washed with 200 mmol/L NaCl and Milli-Q water. The multi-phosphopeptide enrichment procedures were similar to the traditional strategies [16]. Briefly, the peptides mixture was incubated with phosphate ion pre-treated affinity adsorbents with a ratio of 1:10 (w/w) for 30 min in the sample loading buffer (40% acetonitrile (v/v) and 3% TFA (v/v)). Then the non-specific adsorbed peptides on the adsorbents surfaces were washed away by washing buffer 1 (50% acetonitrile (v/v) 6% TFA (v/v) and 200 mmol/L NaCl) and washing buffer 2 (30% acetonitrile (v/v) and 0.1% TFA (v/v)) in sequential. Finally, the multi-phosphopeptides were eluted from the affinity adsorbents by 10% NH3radical dotH2O (v/v) and lyophilized.
The TiO2 adsorbents (10 μm, 100 Å) were purchased from GL Sciences. 1 mg TiO2 adsorbents were firstly incubated with 200 μL 10 mmol/L phosphate salt solution (pH < 2) for 30 min and stepwise washed with 200 mmol/L NaCl (pH < 2) and 0.1% formic acid in H2O. The detail enrichment procedure was the same as Ti4+-IMAC adsorbents.
2.3. Zeta potential measurement
All the adsorbents surfaces zeta potentials were measured with Malven Zetasizer Nano ZS90 (Malven, Worcester, UK). Briefly, 0.1 mg adsorbents were dispersed in 1 mL Milli-Q water (pH 7) and added into a disposable folded capillary cell. Before analysis, the whole system was equilibrated for 30 s.
2.4. MALDI-TOF analyses
The peptides samples of α-casein were analysed by AB Sciex 5800 MALDI-TOF/TOF mass spectrometer (AB, SCIEX, USA) with a pulsed Nd/YAG laser at 355 nm in the linear positive ion mode. 2,5-Dihydroxybenzoic acid (DHB, 25 mg/mL in 70% acetonitrile (v/v) solution containing 1% H3PO4 (v/v)) was selected as matrix for the analysis of the peptides. Briefly, 0.5 μL of sample was firstly dropped onto the MALDI plate and dried at room temperature, followed with addition of 0.5 μL matrix. Then, this plate was injected into the mass spectrometer for analyses.
2.5. LC–MS/MS analyses
The HepG2 phosphopeptides samples were analyzed with Q Exactive mass spectrometer coupled with UltiMate™ 3000 RSLCnano System (Thermo, San Jose, CA). The samples were firstly dissolved into 0.1% formic acid (FA, v/v) solution (Buffer A) and loaded onto a 3 cm × 200 μm I.D. trap column (C18 AQ, Sunchrom, 5 μm, 120 Å) with Buffer A at 5 μL/min. The separation was performed onto a 15 cm × 75 μm I.D. C18 column (C18 AQ, Sunchrom, 5 μm, 120 Å) with a binary separation gradient from 5% ∼ 35% Buffer B 0.1% FA in 80% acetonitrile in 90 min. The flow rate was set at 300 nL/min. The MS dataset was collected in a data dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. A resolution of 70000 (resolution at m/z = 200) was applied to the survey scan (MS) in the Orbitrap with a scan range of 350–1600 m/z, then the top 15 ions were subjected to the stepped HCD fragmentation with a normalize collision energy (NEC) of 30% with a step of 10%. The fragment ions (MS/MS) were detected in Orbitrap with a resolution of 35000 (resolution at m/z = 200). The AGC targets for MS and MS/MS were set to 1E6 and 5E5, respectively, and the injection time was both set at 120 ms. The dynamic exclusion was enabled with an exclusion time of 90 s.
2.6. Data analysis
All the data of the LC–MS/MS were searched by Maxquant (version 1.3.0.5) against a human protein database obtained from Uniprot (201312). Trypsin was selected as the specific enzyme with up to 3 miss cleavages. The phosphorylation of serine (S), threonine (T) and tyrosine (Y) and the oxidation of methionine (M) were set as variable modifications. Meanwhile, the carbamidomethylation of cysteine (C) was set as fixed modification. Phosphopeptides with the false discovery rate <0.01 were accepted for confident identifications with a minimum score of 20. The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium via the PRIDE [23] partner repository with the dataset identifier PXD004252.
