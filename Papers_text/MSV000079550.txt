=== TITLE ===
A quantitative proteomics tool to identify DNA-protein interactions in primary cells or blood.

=== ABSTRACT ===
Interactions between transcription factors and genomic DNA, and in particular their impact on disease and cell fate, have been extensively studied on a global level using techniques based on next-generation sequencing. These approaches, however, do not allow an unbiased study of protein complexes that bind to certain DNA sequences. DNA pulldowns from crude lysates combined with quantitative mass spectrometry were recently introduced to close this gap. Established protocols, however, are restricted to cell lines because they are based on metabolic labeling or require large amounts of material. We introduce a high-throughput-compatible DNA pulldown that combines on-bead digestion with direct dimethyl labeling or label-free protein quantification. We demonstrate that our method can efficiently identify transcription factors binding to their consensus DNA motifs in extracts from primary foreskin fibroblasts and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) freshly isolated from human donors. Nuclear proteomes with absolute quantification of nearly 7000 proteins in K562 cells and PBMCs clearly link differential interactions to differences in protein abundance, hence stressing the importance of selecting relevant cell extracts for any interaction in question. As shown for rs6904029, a SNP highly associated with chronic lymphocytic leukemia, our approach can provide invaluable functional data, for example, through integration with GWAS.

=== METHODS ===
Generation of K562 Nuclear Extracts
K562 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 + GlutaMAX-I (Life Technologies) supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. 3.5 L glass reactors containing a stirring unit were inoculated with K562 cells to a density of 1.5 × 106 cells/mL. Media was added over time to keep the cell density between 0.5 × 106 and 2.0 × 106 cells/mL until a total volume of 3.5 L was reached. Cells were then harvested by centrifugation and washed twice with PBS, and nuclei were isolated as described previously. (20) From approximately 5 × 109 cells, we obtained approximately 19 mg of nuclear extract. The aliquoted nuclear extract was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C.
Isolation of Primary Foreskin Fibroblasts and Generation of Nuclear Extracts
A foreskin biopsy from an adolescent donor was washed with sterile PBS, covered with Chang Medium D (Irvine scientific) containing 2% pen/strep, and cut into pieces of 1–2 mm in size. The fragmented biopsy was transferred into a T25 flask and incubated for 5–7 days in a tissue culture incubator at 37 °C (5% CO2) without moving the flask. After a week of incubation, fibroblasts started to grow out of the attached foreskin fragments. The flask was split in two at 50% confluency. After another week of growth, Chang medium was replaced with normal growth medium (DMEM + 10% FBS + 1% pen/strep + 2 nM l-glutamine + 10 nM Hepes). Cells were expanded for seven passages, which resulted in a total of 3 × 108 cells. They were washed twice with PBS, and nuclei were isolated as described in ref 20. We obtained approximately 2.6 mg of nuclear extract. The aliquoted nuclear extract was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C.
Isolation of Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells and Generation of Nuclear Extracts
PBMCs were obtained upon Ficoll isolation via a Ficoll-Paque PLUS gradient (GE Healthcare) from two buffy coats, corresponding to 1 L of fresh blood, from two voluntary Dutch donors. This resulted in a total of 1.4 × 109 PBMCs. They were washed twice with PBS, and nuclei were isolated as described in ref 20. From this procedure, 7.5 mg of nuclear extract was obtained. The nuclear extract was aliquoted, snap frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C.
Design of Oligonucleotides
Oligonucleotides containing either the SP1 or GATA1 recognition motif were designed based on an existing genomic region that showed the highest footprint occupancy scores in published K562 digital genomic footprinting data. (21) Oligonucleotides of approximately 50–60 bp containing the motif or a mutated version thereof were synthesized by biomers.net. The forward oligonucleotides contained a 5′-biotin-TEG tag, and both the forward and reverse complement were HPLC-purified. The RUNX1 oligonucleotides were designed according to Mandoli et al. (22) For rs6904029 a region of 25 bp up- and downstream of the SNP was selected. Table 1 contains the nucleotide sequences used.

DNA Pulldown and On-Bead Digestion
DNA pulldowns based on dimethyl labeling were performed in duplicate. DNA pulldowns based on label-free quantification were performed in triplicate. Biotinylated forward and 1.5 times the amount of complementary reverse oligonucleotides in a buffer containing 10 mM HEPES, pH 8.0, 50 mM NaCl, and 1 mM EDTA were annealed in a standard PCR machine by heating the mixture at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by a decrease in temperature at 0.1 °C/s until 4 °C. For each experiment, four (dimethyl) or six (label-free) wells of a Multiscreen filter plate with 1.2 μm pores (Millipore, MSBVN1210) were incubated with 50 μL of 70% ethanol for 1 min. The solution was removed by applying vacuum using a Multiscreen vacuum manifold (Millipore). The wells were washed twice with 200 μL of DNA-binding buffer (DBB) containing 10 mM HEPES, pH 8.0, 1 M NaCl, 10 mM EDTA, and 0.05% NP40. Twenty microliters slurry/10 μL beads of high-performance streptavidin sepharose (GE Healthcare, 17511301) was added to each well together with 200 μL of DBB, and the wells were washed once more with 200 μL of DBB. Subsequently, 50 pmol of annealed oligonucleotides (2 or 3× motif containing, 2 or 3× mutated motif containing) in 150 μL of DBB was added to the wells, and they were incubated 1 h at room temperature by shaking at 600 rpm on a plate shaker. For oligonucleotide titration experiments, in addition to 50 pmol, also 10, 100, 250, and 500 pmol of dsOligos was added to the standard amount of 20 μL slurry beads. Subsequent steps were all carried out at 4 °C. The beads with immobilized oligonucleotides were washed twice with DBB and twice with protein binding buffer (PBB) containing 50 mM HEPES, pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% NP40, and 1 mM DTT. Two-hundred fifty micrograms of nuclear extract and 10 μg of competitor DNA in 200 μL of PBB supplemented with EDTA-free complete protease inhibitors (Roche) was then added to each experiment and incubated for 1.5 h with shaking at 4 °C. For nuclear extract titration experiments, in addition to 250 μg, also 10, 50, 100, and 500 μg of nuclear extract was added to the beads with different amounts of immobilized oligonucleotides. The beads were then washed three times with PBB and three times with wash buffer (WB) containing 100 mM TEAB and 150 mM NaCl to remove the detergent present in the PBB. To reduce the proteins, 50 μL of 100 mM TEAB containing 5 mM TCEP was added to the beads and incubated for 60 min at 37 °C in a humidified incubator. Ten millimolar MMTS was added for alkylation and incubated for 10 min at room temperature. Proteins were digested on-bead by adding 0.4 μg of a trypsin/LysC mix (Promega). After incubation for 1 h at 37 °C in a humidified incubator, predigested proteins were collected into a collection plate, and the filter plate was washed once with 50 μL of 100 mM TEAB into the same collection plate. Digestion was continued at 37 °C overnight in a humidified incubator. The next morning, samples for label-free quantification were acidified by adding 5 μL of TFA and purified on stage-tips as described previously. (23)
Dimethyl Labeling
Dimethyl labeling was based on the original protocol published by the Heck lab. (16) After digestion of proteins overnight, samples for dimethyl labeling were not acidified and purified, but dimethyl labeling reagents were added directly to the well. Four microliters of 4% CH2O (light) or CD2O (heavy) together with 4 μL of 0.6 M NaBH3CN was incubated with the sample for 1 h at room temperature, with shaking. The reaction was stopped by adding 16 μL of 1% NH3. Corresponding light and heavy labeled samples were merged and acidified with 10 μL of TFA before stage-tip purification. Each pulldown was performed twice, switching the labels.
Nuclear Proteomes of K562 and PBMCs
Sample preparation for absolute quantification of proteins in K562 and PBMC nuclear extracts was done as described previously. (8, 24) In short, 3.3 μg of universal protein standard 2 (UPS2, Sigma-Aldrich) was mixed with 10 μg of K562 or PBMC nuclear extract, and proteins were digested using the filter-aided sample preparation (FASP) procedure. (25) In addition, 30 μg of nuclear extract were digested using FASP, followed by separation in 6 fractions using strong anion exchange. (25) All samples were purified on C18 stage-tips. (23)
Mass Spectrometric Analysis
Samples were eluted from the stage-tips with 2 × 20 μL of 50% acetonitrile/50% water/0.1% formic acid, and the acetonitrile was evaporated by reducing the sample volume to 5 μL using a SpeedVac centrifuge. After adding 10 μL of 0.1% formic acid in water, 5 μL of the sample was loaded onto a 30 cm column packed in-house with 1.8 μm Reprosil-Pur C18-AQ (Dr. Maisch, 9852). The sample was separated during a gradient from 7 to 32% solvent B (80% acentonitrile/20% water/0.1% formic acid) in solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water) over 94 min (for pulldowns) or 239 min (for proteomes/SAX fractions) using an Easy-nLC 1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The column was heated at 40 °C using a column oven (Sonation). Eluting peptides were sprayed directly into a QExactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The mass spectrometer was operated in TOP10 data-dependent acquisition. Target values for full MS were set to 3 000 000 ions and maximum injection time to 20 ms. Full MS were recorded at a resolution of 70 000 at m/z = 400 and a scan range of 300–1650 m/z. Target values for MS/MS were set to 10 000 ions with a maximum injection time of 120 ms. MS/MS spectra were recorded at a resolution of 17 500. The isolation width was set to 3.0 m/z, and the collision energy, to NCE = 25. Dynamic exclusion was enabled for 20 s, peptides with single or unknown charge state were excluded for sequencing, and the underfill ratio was set to 0.1%.
Raw Data Analysis
Raw MS spectra were analyzed using the MaxQuant software suite (version 1.3.5.7) with standard settings. (26) For dimethyl samples, an additional mass of 28.03 Da (light) or 32.06 Da (heavy) at the N-terminus and lysines was specified as labels. For label-free pulldown samples, the match between runs option as well as the LFQ option was enabled. Methylthiol (Δm = 45.99) or carbamidomethylation was specified as a fixed modification on cysteines for MMTS or iodoacetamide treated samples, respectively. Acetylation of N-termini and oxidation of methionines was allowed as variable modification. Trypsin/P was selected as specific enzyme and two missed cleavages were allowed. Data was searched against the human UniProt database (downloaded 13.06.2012) using the integrated Andromeda search engine. For UPS2 containing samples, a database containing identifiers for all 48 proteins (without quantities) was added in addition. The search was performed with a mass tolerance of 4.5 ppm mass accuracy for the precursor ion and 20 ppm for fragment ions. Peptides and proteins were both accepted at an FDR of 0.01. For quantification, at least two ratio counts were required.
Analysis of Pulldown Samples
Both dimethyl and label-free plots were generated using Perseus and R essentially as we described previously for protein–protein interactions using SILAC or label-free quantification. (17) In short, protein lists were filtered for contaminants and reverse hits. Proteins were required to have 2 peptides, of which at least 1 was unique, to be considered as identified. For dimethyl experiments, the outlier significance was calculated based on protein intensity (significance B) and was required to be <0.001 for both the forward and reverse experiments. (26) A two-dimensional plot was generated in R, and significant outliers were labeled with their corresponding gene names. For label-free experiments, outliers were determined based on t-test statistics. Volcano plots including outlier lines were generated using R, and proteins with an FDR < 0.001 were labeled with their corresponding gene name.
Absolute Quantification of Nuclear Extract Proteomes
Absolute quantification of proteins in K562 and PBMC nuclear extracts was done as described previously. (8, 24) In short, a linear regression based on the absolute amounts of UPS2 proteins and their corresponding iBAQ values was performed. UPS2 proteins with less than 2 unique peptides were excluded, and only proteins in the highest four abundance orders of magnitude were considered. Subsequently, all proteins in the UPS2 containing digest were quantified based on their iBAQ values and the linear regression of UPS2 proteins. A second linear regression based on absolute protein amounts in the UPS2–containing sample and their corresponding iBAQ values in the FASP-SAX nuclear proteome was then performed. Each point of the plot was weighed based on its density for the linear regression. Subsequently, all proteins in the nuclear proteomes could be assigned with absolute amounts reported in fmol/μg nuclear extract. All values can be found in Supporting Information Table 4. Plots and linear regressions were done in R.
Analysis of RNA Sequencing Data from K562 and Primary Foreskin Fibroblasts
RNA sequencing reads mapped to the human genome (build hg19) for K562 cells and primary FSF were downloaded from GEO (GEO accession nos. GSM591666 and GSM751277). (27, 28) Data was analyzed using a script written in-house (RNaseq2RefSeq pl) as follows: (9) For each annotated transcript (RefSeq), mapped reads are intersected with the corresponding exons and counted. For each transcript, all counted reads are then summed, and a reads per kilobase per million mapped reads (RPKM) value is calculated.
