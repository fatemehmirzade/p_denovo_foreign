=== TITLE ===
On-column trypsinization allows for re-use of matrix in modified multiplexed inhibitor beads assay

=== ABSTRACT ===
The Multiplexed Inhibitor Bead (MIB) assay is a previously published quantitative proteomic MS-based approach to study cellular kinomes. A rather extensive procedure, need for multiple custom-made kinase inhibitors and an inability to re-use the MIB-columns, has limited its applicability. Here we present a modified MIB assay in which elution of bound proteins is facilitated by on-column trypsinization. We tested the modified MIB assay by analyzing extract from three human cancer cell lines treated with the cytotoxic drugs cisplatin or docetaxel. Using only three immobilized kinase inhibitors, we were able to detect about 6000 proteins, including ∼40% of the kinome, as well as other signaling, metabolic and structural proteins. The method is reproducible and the MIB-columns are re-usable without loss of performance. This makes the MIB assay a simple, affordable, and rapid assay for monitoring changes in cellular signaling.

=== METHODS ===
2.1. Chemicals
Purvalanol B (PurvB) and Bisindolylmaleimide X (BisX), were from Tocris Bioscience and Alexis Corporation, respectively. (R)-3-(4-((1-Phenylethyl)amino)thieno [2,3-d]pyrimidin-6-yl)benzoic acid (SB6-060-05) was prepared as previously described [6].
2.2. Cell lines
JJN-3 (multiple myeloma) and H460 (NCI-460; large cell lung cancer) were grown in RPMI-1640 medium (Sigma-Aldrich). The cell lines A549 (ATCC CCL-185; non-small cell lung cancer) and U2OS (ATCC HTB-96; osteosarcoma) were grown in DMEM (Sigma-Aldrich). Both media were supplemented with fetal bovine serum (10%), amphotericin B (2.5 μg/mL), L-glutamine (2 mM, all from Sigma-Aldrich) and penicillin (100 units/mL)-streptomycin (0.1 mg/mL) (Gibco, NY, USA). Cells were cultivated in a humidified atmosphere (95% air, 5% CO2, 37 °C).
2.3. Viability assay
Cell growth over time was measured using the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5 diphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT, Sigma-Aldrich). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates (3000 cells/well) and continuously exposed to various doses of docetaxel (1–25 ng/mL) or cisplatin (1–10 μM for A549 and U2OS, 0.1–1 μM for H460) until harvest at day one, three and four. MTT was added at each day of harvest 4 h prior to absorbance measurements at 565 nm. Data shown are average of 6 wells ± S.D. in one representative experiment out of two.
2.4. Cell extracts
Cells were treated with docetaxel (5 ng/mL) or cisplatin (5 μM for A549 and U2OS, 0.5 μM for H460) for 24 h and harvested by scraping in ice-cold PBS. The cells were washed once, pelleted, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until use. For the lysis, the samples were thawed and mixed with 3 × packed cell volume of lysis buffer (50 mM HEPES-NaOH pH 7.5, 0.5% Triton X-100, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1 mM EGTA, 10 mM NaF, 2.5 mM Na3VO4 (sodium orthovanadate), and 1X Halt™ protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA)). Samples were sonicated for 2 min on ice prior to centrifugation (15 min, 14,000 rpm, 4 °C) and collection of the supernatant that was used in the MIB assays and for western analysis.
2.5. Western blot analysis
Samples of cell extracts were denatured in NuPAGE LDS sample buffer with 10 mM dithiothreitol by incubating at 95 °C for 10 min. This solution was used for technical replicates of the blots. Proteins were separated by polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (10% Bis-Tris gels, NuPAGE, Invitrogen, CA, USA) and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Immobilon, Millipore, Ireland). Membranes were blocked in blocking buffer (5% fat-free dry milk in PBS) before incubation with primary antibodies against AKT1/2 (#4691), Phospho-AKT1/2 (P-Akt, #4060, Ser473),, Phospho-MAPK3/1 (ERK1/2) (P-ERK, #4370, Thr202/Tyr204), RPS6KA5 (p70 S6 Kinase) (S6K, #2708), pRPS6KA5 (Phospho-S6K) (Psingle bondS6K, #9206, Thr389), cleaved Caspase 3 (#9662, all from Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), MAPK3/1 (ERK1/2) (ERK, sc-93-G, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, TX, USA), and β-tubulin (ab6046, Abcam, UK). The fluorescently labelled secondary antibodies goat α-rabbit 680RD, goat α-mouse 800 C W and donkey α-goat 800 C W (all Li-Cor Biosciences, UK) were used for protein detection. All antibodies were diluted in blocking buffer with 0.1% Tween 20. The proteins were visualized in Odyssey infrared imaging system (LI-COR Biosciences, UK) and quantified in Odyssey Image Studio (V5.2.5). Protein levels were normalized against β-tubulin and compared to untreated control.
2.6. Multiplexed inhibitor beads (MIB)
Three different kinase inhibitors were immobilized using ECH Sepharose 4B (Bisindolylmaleimide X) or EAH Sepharose 4B (Purvalanol B and SB6-060-05) beads (GE Healthcare, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions (and as published elsewhere) [2]. The beads were washed and stored in 20% ethanol until used. 300–500 μL of mixed inhibitor beads (settled bead volume) was used per column (high recovery, BioRad). 100 μL of cell extract (1–5 mg/mL protein) was added per column, incubated for up to 10 min at room temperature and then slowly released under minimal column flow to maximize sample contact with the beads. The MIB-columns were then washed to remove unbound components as follows: 1 mL 150 mM NaCl, 1 mL of 0.1% SDS, 1 mL of deionized water, 1 mL of 1 M NaCl, 1 mL 150 mM NaCl and finally 1 mL 100 mM NH4HCO3. Then the flow was stopped and 100 μL of freshly made 200 mM NH4HCO3 was added together with 5 μL of 100 ng/μL MS-grade trypsin (Thermo Scientific, USA). At this point the matrix and the trypsin solution were thoroughly mixed by pipetting, and the reaction mixtures incubated at room temperature overnight with occasional stirring.
2.7. Sample preparation for MS
MIB-columns were eluted with 0.5 mL of 100 mM NH4HCO3 and 0.5 mL of acetonitrile, both of which were collected and dried. Dried peptides were resuspended in 100 μL 0.1% formic acid. Residual detergent was removed using detergent removal 0.5 mL spin columns (Thermo Pierce, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The peptides were then desalted in pipet tip C18 columns, eluted with acetonitrile, dried and dissolved in 35 μL 0.1% formic acid for MS.
2.8. Wash and reuse of MIB-columns
After use, MIB-column matrices were washed stepwise using 0.5 mL acetonitrile, 1.5 mL deionized water, 1.5 mL 20% SDS, 3 mL deionized water, 3 mL 5 M NaCl, 10 mL deionized water and finally 3 mL 150 mM NaCl. Columns were stored in 20% ethanol at 4 °C until reuse. The MIB-columns were re-equilibrated with NaCl prior to next usage.
2.9. LC-MS/MS analysis
LC-MS/MS analysis was performed using an EASY-nLC 1000 UPLC system (Thermo Scientific, USA) interfaced with a Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, USA) via a nanospray ESI ion source (Thermo Scientific, USA). 10 μL of the peptide solution was injected onto an Acclaim PepMap100 C-18 trap column (75 μm i.d. 2 cm, C18, 3 μm, 100 Å, Thermo Scientific, USA) and an acclaim PepMap100 C-18 analytical column (75 μm i.d. 50 cm, 2 μm, 100 Å, Thermo Scientific, USA) using a 120 min multi-step gradient (5 min 2%–6% B, 26 min 6%–12% B, 49 min 12%–20% B, 15 min 20%–28% B, 7 min 28% −4 0% B, 3 min 40%–100% B, 15 min at 100% B; where B is 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile and A is 0.1% formic acid in water) at 250 nL/min. Peptides were analyzed in positive ion mode under data dependent acquisition (DDA) using the following parameters: Electrospray voltage 1.9 kV, HCD fragmentation with normalized collision energy 30, automatic gain control target value of 3 × 106 for Orbitrap MS and 1 × 105 for MS/MS scans. Each MS scan (400–1600 m/z) was acquired at a resolution of 70,000 FWHM, followed by 10 MS/MS scans with isolation window 4.0 m/z, triggered for intensities above 1.7 × 104, at a maximum ion injection time of 100 ms for MS and 60 ms for MS/MS scans. A dynamic exclusion of 40 s was used as well as charge exclusion for unassigned, 1, and greater than 4.
2.10. Mass spectrometry data analysis
The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium via the PRIDE [8] partner repository with the dataset identifiers PXD005286 and PXD005402. Thermo Sieve ™ was used to align MSMS spectra. Preview 2.3.5 (Protein Metrics Inc.) was used to determine optimal search criteria [9]. These were plugged in Max Quant v 1.5.30 [10] mapping the spectra over Human canonical proteome with isoforms (Uniprot March 2016) [11]. The following search parameters were used: enzyme specified as trypsin with maximum two missed cleavages allowed; deamidation of asparagine/glutamine, oxidation of methionine, N-terminal acetylation, and dimethylation of lysine/arginine as variable modifications. Precursor mass tolerance was set to 20 ppm with fragment mass tolerance of 0.02 Da. False discovery rate was set to 0.01 (high confidence) for peptide as well as protein group identification. Label free quantification (LFQ) algorithm [12] was used to estimate the protein amounts in the sample using match between the runs with alignment time of 20 min and match time window of 1 min. This option was disabled for comparing three technical replicates. These LFQ values were log transformed with base 2 and the transformed control values were subtracted. The resulting values reflecting the change relative to control for each condition were subjected to two sided non-parametric Wilcoxon Sign Rank test [13] as implemented in MATLAB R2015a (MathWorks Inc.) in order to check the consistency in directionality of the change, namely a negative sign reflecting decreased and positive sign reflecting increased expression of respective protein group. The choice of this non-parametric test avoids the assumption of certain type of null distribution as in Student's t-test by working over the Rank of the observation instead of observation value itself. Furthermore, it also makes it robust to outliers and extreme variations noticed in observed values. Only groups with p-value < 0.25 were considered for further analysis.
