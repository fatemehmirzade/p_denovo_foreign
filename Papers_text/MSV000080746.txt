=== TITLE ===
Ectodomain shedding of CD99 within highly conserved regions is mediated by the metalloprotease meprin β and promotes transendothelial cell migration

=== ABSTRACT ===
The adhesion molecule CD99 is essential for the transendothelial migration of leukocytes. In this study, we used biochemical and cellular assays to show that CD99 undergoes ectodomain shedding by the metalloprotease meprin β and subsequent intramembrane proteolysis by γ-secretase. The cleavage site in CD99 was identified by mass spectrometry within an acidic region highly conserved through different vertebrate species. This finding fits perfectly to the unique cleavage specificity of meprin β with a strong preference for aspartate residues and suggests coevolution of protease and substrate. We hypothesized that limited CD99 cleavage by meprin β would alter cellular transendothelial migration (TEM) behavior in tissue remodeling processes, such as inflammation and cancer. Indeed, meprin β induced cell migration of Lewis lung carcinoma cells in an in vitro TEM assay. Accordingly, deficiency of meprin β in Mep1b−/− mice resulted in significantly increased CD99 protein levels in the lung. Therefore, meprin β could serve as a therapeutic target, given that in a proof-of-concept approach we showed accumulation of CD99 protein in lungs of meprin β inhibitor-treated mice.—Bedau, T., Peters, F., Prox, J., Arnold, P., Schmidt, F., Finkernagel, M., Köllmann, S., Wichert, R., Otte, A., Ohler, A., Stirnberg, M., Lucius, R., Koudelka, T., Tholey, A., Biasin, V., Pietrzik, C. U., Kwapiszewska, G., Becker-Pauly, C. Ectodomain shedding of CD99 within highly conserved regions is mediated by the metalloprotease meprin β and promotes transendothelial cell migration. === METHODS ===
Chemicals

All chemicals were of analytical grade and were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (Munich, Germany), Carl Roth (Karlsruhe, Germany), Merck (Darmstadt, Germany), Roche Diagnostics (Mannhein, Germany), or Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA).

Animals

The generation of Mep1b−/− mice has been described in Norman et al. (33). Mice were kept in specific pathogen-free conditions in isolated ventilated cages, with a 12-h light-dark cycle and food and water provided ad libitum. Our investigations were performed in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (German Animal Welfare Act on Protection of Animals). All animal protocols were approved by the Central Animal Facility of the University of Kiel and the relevant German authorities.

Treatment of mice with actinonin

Ten C57BL/6 mice (9 wk of age) were randomized into groups with similar average body weights (20–22 g). Actinonin (A6671; Sigma-Aldrich), at a concentration of0.5 mg/kgbodyweight, or 0.9% isotonic saline solution (154 mM NaCl) with 5% ethanol as the control, was administered intraperitoneally twice a day for 1 wk. Afterward, the mice were anesthetized with ketanest (800 mg/kg) and medetomidine (5 mg/kg), flushed, and euthanized. Lungs were taken and proteins from homogenates were analyzed by Western blot. The animal study was conducted in compliance with European and German guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, and were approved by the Central Animal Facility of the University of Mainz and the Ethics Committee on Animal Care and Use of Rhineland-Palatinate (G12-1-017).

Cells, antibodies, plasmids, and transfection

The following antibodies were used: polyclonal anti-human CD99 (AF3968; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), monoclonal anti-human CD99 (12E7, sc-53148; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA), polyclonal anti-murine CD99 (AF3905; R&D Systems), polyclonal anti-murine CD99 (provided by Dietmar Vestweber, Max Planck Institute of Molecular Biomedicine, Münster, Germany), polyclonal anti-murine CD99 (M-12, sc-241355; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), anti-human meprin β (self-made polyclonal IgG; a gift from Erwin Sterchi, University of Bern, Bern, Switzerland), 2 polyclonal anti-meprin β (self-made, Pineda Antibody-Service, Berlin, Germany), monoclonal antimyc (9B11, 2276; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), monoclonal anti-FLAG (M2, F1804; Sigma-Aldrich), polyclonal anti-transferrin receptor (ab84036; Abcam, Cambridge, United Kingdom), monoclonal anti-GAPDH (2118,14C10; Cell Signaling Technology), and polyclonal anti-actin (A2066; Sigma-Aldrich). All cells [HeLa, Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC), human embryonic kidney (HEK)293T, and bEnd.3 cells] were grown in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS), glutamine, and penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were cultured at 37°C, 5% CO2 atmosphere and 95% relative humidity. The transfection procedure with polyethylenimine (PEI) was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions. For expression of human CD99 mammalian expression vector pCMV6-Entry with C-terminal myc-DDK tag (RC204056 and RC207079; Origene, Rockville, MD, USA) was used. Meprin β and its inactive variant (E153A) were expressed by using constructs kindly provided by Erwin Sterchi (34). Empty pcDNA3.1(—) was used as the vector control.

Recombinant proteins

The recombinant meprin β protein was produced using the Bac-to-Bac baculovirus expression system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and purified via affinity chromatography (35). Recombinant human CD99 Fc chimera protein was obtained from R&D Systems (3968-CD-050).

Treatment of cells with recombinant meprin β

Treatment of various cell lines with recombinant soluble meprin β was used to accurately study time- and concentration-dependent effects. Before they were harvested, the cells were washed with PBS to remove residual FCS-containing medium, DMEM (without supplements) was added, and finally meprin β was added at the desired concentration for a defined period of time. In experiments where the inhibitor actinonin was used, meprin β was preincubated with actinonin in serum-free DMEM for 15 min at room temperature.

Cell lysis, SDS-PAGE, and Western blot

At 36–48 h after transfection, the cells were harvested and lysed [120 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris-HCl, 1% Triton X-100, complete proteinase inhibitor cocktail (Roche); pH 7.4]. Bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein quantification was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE, transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (GE Healthcare, Munich, Germany), and blocked for 1 h at room temperature with 5% milk in Tris-buffered saline-Tween (TBS-T). After primary antibody incubation overnight at 4°C, the blots were washed 4 times with TBS-T, incubated with horseradish peroxidase–conjugated secondary antibodies in TBS-T for 1 h at room temperature, washed again, and eventually developed with ECL Ultra substrate (Lumigen, Southfield, MI, USA) and a chemoluminescence detection system (LAS-3000; Fujifilm, Düsseldorf, Germany).

Peptide cleavage assay and HPLC analysis

A synthetic peptide representing the putative meprin-β cleavage site within the CD99 sequence (SSGSFSDADLADGVSGGE) was obtained from Genosphere Biotechnologies (Paris, France). Reversed-phase (RP)-HPLC of the full-length peptide was performed with a gradient from 2% acetonitrile/0.1% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) in H2O up to 60% acetonitrile (ACN)/0.1% TFA for 120 min with detection at 214 nm. For cleavage analysis, 100 µl of peptide solution (500 µM) was incubated with 15 nM recombinant meprin β for 1 h at room temperature. Digestion was stopped by the addition of 1900 ml of 0.1% TFA before chromatography, and peak fraction collection of the cleaved peptide fragments was performed with the same gradient described above for 65 min (Supplemental Fig. 1A). For all HPLC experiments, the LC-20AD double plunger, FRC-10A fraction collector, and SPD-M20A detector (all Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) were used.

Identification of cleavage sites from HPLC fractions: matrix-assisted desorption/ionization MS

Each LC fraction (250 ml) was dried via vacuum centrifugation to concentrate the sample. The dried samples were reconstituted in 25 ml of 3% ACN in 0.1% TFA, and 1 ml of the sample was then spotted onto a matrix-assisted desorption/ionization (MALDI) target in duplicate and left to air dry. MALDI matrix (3 mg/ml of α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid) in 70% ACN/0.1% TFA was then applied to the dried spots. MALDI tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) analysis was performed with a 5800 MALDI-time of flight (TOF) mass spectrometer (Sciex, Darmstadt, Germany). Measurement conditions were as follows. The reflectron positive ion mode was used; 2000 laser shots per spot were acquired for MS spectra, and a 1-point recalibration was performed with a matrix peak of m/z 877.034. For MS/MS experiments, 2500 shots were averaged with a pulse rate of 1000 Hz. Precursor ions were separated by timed ion selection with a 300 resolution window (full width at half maximum). Peptides were fragmented with either postsource decay or collision-induced dissociation (CID) with deceleration to an energy of 1 kV. For CID experiments, ambient air was used as the collision gas, with medium pressure of 10−6 torr. Fragmentation spectra were analyzed with the ion-fragmentation calculator tool that is embedded in the Data Explorer software 4.10 (Sciex) and manually inspected. Theoretical peptide masses and peptide mass deviations were calculated with Mmass (version 2.4; http://www/mmass.org).

Identification of cleavage sites from cell lysates: SDS-PAGE, reductive dimethylation, and in-gel digestion

CD99 C terminus was immunoprecipitated from HeLa cell lysates transfected with myc/FLAG-tagged CD99 and untagged meprin β, with an anti-FLAG antibody. The precipitate was separated by SDS-PAGE (12% gel) and stained with Coomassie blue. Bands (Supplemental Fig. 1B) were cut using a scalpel, according to signals detected in a control Western blot (Supplemental Fig. 1C). Gel bands were then cut into smaller cubes (2 mm3) and destained with 50 mM 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfonic acid (HEPES; pH 8.0), followed by 50 mM HEPES (pH 8.0), in 30% (v/v) ACN. Samples were dehydrated (100% ACN) and dried using vacuum centrifugation before being reduced and alkylated with 10 mM DTT (56°C for 45 min) and 55 mM iodoacetamide (30 min at room temperature) in 100 mM HEPES (pH 8.0), respectively. Samples were dehydrated (100% ACN) and dried by vacuum centrifugation, followed by overnight reductive dimethylation of protein amino groups (protein N-terminal and lysine residues) by using 40 mM formaldehyde in 40 mM NaCNBH3 in 100 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.5) at 25°C. The reaction was quenched by removing the reaction buffer and adding ammonium bicarbonate (ABC; 100 mM, 1 h) followed by formic acid (FA; 0.1%; 1 h). Samples were washed in the reagents used for the destaining procedure described above, with the exception that HEPES was replaced with ABC buffer. Samples were dehydrated by adding ACN, followed by vacuum centrifugation before digestion. Digestion was performed in parallel with 50 ng of chymotrypsin and 25 ng trypsin overnight in 10 mM HEPES buffer (2 mM CaCl2;pH7.5) at 37°C. Peptides were extracted from the gel pieces with 1% FA, 1% FA in 50% ACN (v/v), and 100% ACN with sonication Pooled sample extracts were dried via vacuum centrifugation, frozen at −20°C, and resuspended in LC-MS loading buffer (3% ACN/0.1% TFA) before LC-MS analysis.

Identification of cleavage sites from cell lysates: LC-electrospray ionization MS/MS analysis and data interpretation

LC-electrospray ionization-MS/MS analyses were performed starting with low-molecular-weight samples (from gels, Supplemental Fig. 1B). Each run was followed by a blank run to minimize sample carryover. Nano-UHPLC-MS was performed on an UltiMate 3000 RSL Nano/Cap System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled online to an Orbitrap-Q Exactive Plus (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Samples were desalted for 4 min (Acclaim PepMap100 C-18, 300 µm inner diameter × 5 mm, 5 µm, 100 Å; Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a flow rate of 30 ml/min using 3% ACN/0.1% TFA. An Acclaim PepMap 100 C-18 column (75 µm inner diameter × 500 mm, 2 µm, 100 Å; Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for analytical separation at a flow rate of 300 nl/min using binary gradients of buffers A (0.05% FA) and B (80% ACN and 0.04% FA). The elution used gradient steps of 5–50% B (4–30 min) and 50–90% B (30–35 min), followed by an isocratic wash (90% B, 35–45 min) and column re-equilibration (5% B, 45–60 min) steps. Samples were analyzed on the Q-Exactive from low to high molecular weight.

MS scans were acquired in the mass range of 300–2000 m/z at a resolution of 70,000. The 10 most intense signals were subjected to high-energy collision-induced dissociation (HCD) using a dynamic exclusion of 15 s. MS/MS parameters: minimum signal intensity, 1000; isolation width, 3.0 Da; charge state, $2; HCD resolution, 15,000; and normalized collision energy of 25. The lock mass (445.120025) was used for data acquired in MS mode. HCD spectra were searched with Proteome Discoverer 1.4 (1.4.0.288; Thermo Fisher Scientific) with the Sequest-HT search algorithm against the reviewed and canonical human database with contaminants (ftp://ftp.thegpm.org/fasta/cRAP/) appended to the database (20,319 sequences). The following database search settings were used: MS tolerance, ±10 ppm; MS2 tolerance, 0.02 Da; enzyme specificity, none; and fixed modification at lysine (dimethylation) and cysteine (carbamidomethylation) residues. Modification at the peptide N terminus (dimethylation) was regarded as a variable. Only peptides that were identified with high [false-discovery rate (FDR) < 1%] or medium (FDR < 5%) confidence were included. LC-MS proteomics data were deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium (36) via the Proteomics Identifications (PRIDE) partner repository with the dataset identifier PXD005428.

Identification of cleavage sites by Edman degradation

For N-terminal sequencing, proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE, blotted onto PVDF membranes for Edman degradation, stained with Coomassie brilliant blue, and then analyzed at the Protein Microsequencing Centre of the Institut Federatif de Recherche 128 (Lyon, France).

Cell surface protein biotinylation

HeLa cells were transiently transfected with CD99, meprin β, and inactive meprin β (E153A) or empty vector (mock). After 48 h, the cells were cooled down to 4°C, washed with PBS-conditioned medium (CM; 0.1 mM CaCl2 and 1 mM MgCl2 in PBS), and incubated with biotin solution [1 mg/ml Sulfo-NHS-SS-Biotin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in PBS-CM] for 30 min at 4°C. Afterward the cells were incubated with quenching buffer [50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) in PBS-CM] for 10 min and washed with PBS-CM. They were lysed [50 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 1% (v/v) Triton-X 100, 0.1% (w/v) SDS, complete protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche)], and the amount of total protein was determined by BCA protein assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Lysate control for Western blot analyses was taken, and the residual lysate (<600 mg total protein) was incubated with 75 µl streptavidin agarose resin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) on a rotator overnight at 4°C. The agarose was then washed with lysis buffer, mixed with 3× reducing sample buffer, and heated at 60°C for 20 min. Lysates and streptavidin-bound fractions were analyzed by Western blot.

ELISA

For detection of soluble human CD99 ectodomain in cell culture supernatants, an ELISA was performed with a matched antibody pair (H00004267-AP51; Abnova, Taipei City, Taiwan). For detection, a horseradish peroxidase–conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H+L; 31460; Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used. Recombinant human CD99 Fc chimera (3968-CD-050; R&D Systems) served as the protein standard.

Immunofluorescence microscopy

HeLa cells were seeded onto coverslips in 6-well plates and transfected as described above. Twenty-four hours after transfection, cells were washed twice with cold PBS and fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde in PBS for 10 min at room temperature. The cells were then incubated with 0.12% (w/v) glycine in PBS for 10 min at room temperature and blocked and permeabilized with 10% (v/v) FCS and 0.2% (w/v) saponin in PBS for 30 min at room temperature. Primary antibodies were applied in the same solution 1 h at room temperature in a dark, humid chamber [anti-myc, 1:1000; anti-meprin β (hEcto1), 1:1000]. Cells were washed 5 times with 0.2% (w/v) saponin in PBS. Secondary antibody solution was applied for 1 h at room temperature (Alexa Fluor 488 and 594, 1:300; Thermo Fisher Scientific). The coverslips were washed again 5 times with 0.2%(w/v) saponin in PBS and twice in double distilled H2O and then mounted onto slides with Mowiol/1,4-diazabicyclo[2.2.2]octane (DABCO; Sigma-Aldrich) solution [17% (w/v) Mowiol, 33% (v/v) glycerol, and 50 mg/ ml DABCO], including 1 µg/ml DAPI for nuclear staining. A confocal laser scanning microscope (FV1000; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was used to acquire images of the fluorescence-labeled cells.

Immunohistochemistry

Lung tissue of wild-type (WT) and Mep1b−/− mice was dissected; fixed (4% paraformaldehyde in PBS); and, after dehydration, embedded in low-melting-point paraffin according to standard laboratory procedures. Serial sections (7 µm) were cut. Azan trichrome or 3,39-diaminobenzidine (DAB)-staining of serial sections was performed, as previously described (peroxidase substrate kit DAB; Vector Laboratories, Burlingame, CA, USA) (37). The following antibody dilution was used for staining: anti-CD99, M-12, sc-241355, 1:250 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology). After DAB staining, the sections were counterstained with hemalum. As a specificity control for immunostaining, the sections were incubated with the biotinylated secondary antibody.

Quantification of DAB signals from paraffin-embedded sections

All samples compared were stained in the same experiment to exclude differences in staining intensity. Images were recorded with a light microscope (Axiophot; Zeiss, Jena, Germany) with the same white balance and contrast settings for all samples. In a first step, the tissue area was calculated. Therefore, images were binarized and all nonwhite pixels were counted and further processed as tissue. A color reduction was performed to evaluate the DAB intensity. Thereafter, it was possible to discriminate between the blue hemalum and red-brown DAB signal. All signals from the DAB staining were converged to the red color channel and were quantified after separation of the image into red, green, and blue channels. The DAB signal was then put in proportion to the tissue area calculated before. All images were analyzed, with the same thresholds used for all images. The analysis was performed with a bash in-house script that automates commands from ImageMagick (http://www.imagemagick.org).

Flow cytometry

For flow cytometric CD99 surface expression analyses, cells were detached with Accutase (Sigma-Aldrich), resuspended in blocking buffer (PBS, 1% bovine serum albumin), and counted; 1 × 106 cells were incubated for half an hour with a phycoerythrin (PE)-labeled anti-murine (FAB3905P; R&D Systems) or anti-human CD99 antibody (318008; BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA) at 4°C. After excess antibody was washed away, flow cytometric analysis was performed on a FACS Canto System (BD Biosciences, Heidelberg, Germany). Unstained cells or cells incubated with PE IgG2a isotype control antibody (400214; BioLegend) served as negative control. Mean or median PE fluorescence intensity was used for further quantification.

Permeability assay

bEnd.3 cells were detached from 10 cm dishes, the number of cells was determined, and equal amounts of cells (6 × 104 per cm2) were seeded on Transwell Permeable Supports (0.4 mMpolyester membrane, 6.5-mm insert, 24-well plate; Sigma-Aldrich). The cells were cultivated for 3 d in DMEM supplemented with 10% FCS, penicillin/streptomycin, and glutamine until confluence was reached, and a monolayer was formed. To visualize permeability of cells, FITC-conjugated dextran (FD-40, MW 40,000; Sigma-Aldrich) was added to the upper chamber (1 mg/ml). Recombinant meprin β (10 nM final concentration) was added, and permeabilization of the monolayer was followed from 0 to 2 h under standard cell culture conditions. For inhibition experiments, protease-containing samples were pretreated with actinonin at room temperature for 15 min. After different time points, Transwells were removed, and fluorescence in the medium of the bottom chamber was analyzed with a multiplate reader (Tecan, Mannedorf, Switzerland). Monolayers of cultivated bEnd.3 cells treated with FITC dextran served as controls for the integrity of the monolayers over time.

TEM assay

bEnd.3 cells were detached and counted, and 400 µl cell culture medium containing 5 × 105 cells was infused into the upper compartment of the hanging inserts (MCEP12H48; Millicell; EMD Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) placed in a 12-well cell culture plate. The basolateral compartment was filled with 1000 ml cell culture medium, and the cells were grown to confluence for 24 h. Monolayer formation was confirmed by phase-contrast microscopy of a control well without insert, in which the same number of bEnd.3 cells was seeded. At confluence, LLC cells were detached; stained with 10 mM carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE; eBioscience, Frankfurt am Main, Germany), according to the manufacturer's instructions; counted; and resuspended in serum-free DMEM. The hanging cell culture insert membrane with the attached bEnd.3 monolayer was carefully washed with PBS, the inserts were placed into new wells, and the basolateral compartment was filled with 1000 µl serum-free DMEM. LLC cells (5 × 104) in 400 mlserum-freeDMEM were added to the upper compartment. Control cells were incubated for 5 h at 37°C, and meprin β was added to the cells after 1 h (15 nM). Medium in the upper compartment was aspirated, and nonmigrated and endothelial cells were carefully removed from the upper side of the membrane with a sterile cotton swab. CFSE-stained LLC cells that successfully migrated and attached to the bottom side of the membrane were detected with inverted fluorescence microscopy (DMi8; Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). LLC cells that were able to migrate in the absence of bEnd.3 cells served as a positive control. Cells within a 42.25-mm2 area in the center of the membrane were counted with Image J (ver. 1.49; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with Prism 6 software (Graph Pad, La Jolla, CA, USA) for Student's t test or 1-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey's multiple-comparison post hoc test. Values are expressed as means ± sd. The null hypothesis was rejected at P < 0.05.
