=== TITLE ===
Extracellular citrullination inhibits the function of matrix associated TGF-β

=== ABSTRACT ===
In inflammatory arthritis peptidyl arginine deiminase (PAD) enzymes can citrullinate arginine residues in extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins, such as collagens and fibronectin. This may lead to the generation of anti-citrullinated protein antibodies, important diagnostic markers in rheumatoid arthritis. In addition, the citrullination may directly affect protein function. Based on structural analysis, we found that most ECM-associated growth factors (GFs) have arginine residues in their receptor recognition sites. Thus, they are potential functional targets of extracellular citrullination. To examine this further, we focused on the citrullination of transforming growth factor-βs (TGF-β), well-known ECM-associated GFs. PAD-treatment of CHO-LTBP1 cell derived matrix, rich with TGF-β, decreased the level of TGF-β activity as detected by HaCaT and MLEC-PAI-1/Lu reporter cells. Additional experiments indicated that PAD-treatment inhibits the integrin-mediated TGF-β activation since PAD-treatment decreased the binding of integrin αVβ6 ectodomain as well as integrin-mediated spreading of MG-63 and HaCaT cells to β1-latency associated peptide (TGF-β1 LAP). The citrullination of the RGD site, an important integrin recognition motif, was confirmed by mass spectrometry. Furthermore, the citrullination of active TGF-β1 inhibited its binding to recombinant TGF-β receptor II, and prevented its ability to activate TGF-β signaling. Thus, extracellular PAD activity can affect the function of ECM-associated growth factors by different mechanisms. Importantly, the citrullination of both latent and active TGF-β has the potency to regulate the inflammatory process.

=== METHODS ===
Cell cultures and reagents
HaCaT (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA), MG-63 (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA), MLEC-PAI-1/Lu reporter (the kind gift of professor Daniel Rifkin, NYU, School of Medicine, USA) [49], and CHO-LTBP1 (the kind gift of professor Jorma Keski-Oja, University of Helsinki, Finland) [50] cells, as well as primary fibroblast like synoviocytes (the kind gift of Professor Yrjö Konttinen; P5–6 were used in experiments) [40], were growth in DMEM with 10% FCS. When MLEC-PAI-1/Lu and CHO-LTBP1 cells were used G418 was added into the medium (350 μg/ml and 250 μg/ml, respectively).
PAD2 from rabbit skeletal muscle was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, Missouri, United States) and recombinant PAD4-GST was produced and purified as described earlier [40]. TGFβ1-LAP was produced and purified as described earlier [51]. Citrullinations were performed in citrullination buffer (40 mM Tris–HCl, 2 mM or 5 mM CaCl2, pH 7.4). PAD2 and PAD4 inactivated in 1 h treatment at + 37 °C (Supplement Fig. 1E–F). The o/n PAD-treatments were used to reach maximal citrullination but also shorter incubation times were used.
PAD activity assay
Synovial fluid samples were collected in Turku University Hospital (permission number of the ethical committee, ETMK 15/1801/2013). PAD activity of synovial fluids was measured with an assay of fibrinogen citrullination as described earlier [7]. Briefly, wells of 96-well plate wells were coated with fibrinogen in PBS (0.1 mg/ml) o/n at + 4 °C and blocked with BSA (1 mg/ml) 1 h at RT. The synovial fluids were diluted 1/4 with citrullination buffer and incubated on wells o/n at + 37 °C. The amount of citrullinated fibrinogen was measured using anti-citrullinated fibrinogen antibody (Mouse Anti-Citrullinated Fibrinogen (20B2), ModiQuest; 1 h, RT) and Eu-labeled anti-mouse secondary antibody (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts; 1 h, RT). The fluorescence was measured from enhancer filled wells by Victor3 multilabel counter (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts). Wells were washed three times with PBS after each step. PAD2 was used as a control. The dilution of antibodies was 1/1000.
Assays for active and latent TGF-β
Citrullination of TGF-β1 was performed by diluting active TGF-β1 (recombinant human, R&D Systems) with 0.1 mg/ml BSA in citrullination buffer (final concentration 50 ng/ml), adding PAD2 or PAD4 enzyme, and incubating 45 min at + 37 °C. TGF-β1 was measured by seeding 15 000 cells in DMEM (with 10% FCS) to the 96-well plate wells and incubating 5 h in cell incubator. The medium was removed and samples of active TGF-β1 diluted in serum-free DMEM supplemented with 0.1 mg/ml BSA (final concentration of TGF-β1 was 7 ng/ml), were pipetted to the wells. After an 18 h incubation in cell incubator (+ 37 °C, 5% CO2) samples were removed, wells were washed with PBS and Passive lysis buffer (Promega, Fitchburg, Wisconsin, USA) was added. After 15 min incubation at + 37 °C, the Bright-Glo luciferase reagent (Promega, Fitchburg, Wisconsin, USA) was added and the luminescence measured (Victor3 multilabel counter; PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts).
In order to study the citrullination of SLC, recombinant human latent TGF-β1 protein (R&D Systems) was diluted in citrullination buffer (10 μg/ml) and incubated with PAD2 (5 U/ml). After the heat-activation (80 °C, 20 min) [23], the amount of active TGF-β was measured as described above. Latent TGF-β produced by human fibroblast like synoviocytes was studied by collecting conditioned medium (serum-free DMEM after 5 day incubation) and adding PAD2 (2.7 U/ml) and CaCl2 (5 mM) to conditioned medium. After 45 min incubation the samples were heat-activated and active TGF-β was assayed as described above.
Extracellular matrix rich in latent TGF-β1 was produced by growing 50 000 CHO-LTBP1 cells on the wells of a 96 well plate for 72 h. The cells were removed with EDTA treatment (15 mM EDTA in PBS, 45 min at cell incubator). The wells were washed five times with PBS and stored in PBS at + 4 °C max. for 2 weeks. The ECM on the wells was citrullinated by adding PAD2 enzyme in citrullination buffer (1.2 U/ml) and incubating the plate o/n at + 37 °C. The plate was washed three times with PBS. Immediately after washing, 20 000 HaCaT and 15 000 MLEC-PAI-1/Lu reporter cells were added and incubated 18 h in a cell incubator. TGF-β1 was measured as described above. Heat-inactivated PAD2 (+ 98 °C, 30 min) was used as a control.
Cell attachment assays
Plate and wash-assay
The 96-well plates were coated with recombinant TGFβ1-LAP (diluted in PBS, 5 μg/cm2) or BSA (1 mg/ml) at 4 °C overnight. After blocking plates with BSA (1 mg/ml, RT, 1 h), PAD2 was added in citrullination buffer (1.2 U/ml) and incubated o/n at + 37 °C. The wells were washed three times with PBS. Detached HaCaT cells (trypsinized) were seeded to the wells (200 000 per well) in serum-free DMEM. After 30 min incubation (+ 37 °C, 5% CO2), the wells were washed three times and number of attached cells was quantified by WST-1 reagent (Roche).
Real-time spreading assay (xCELLigence)
Plates (96-well E-Plate 96; ACEA Biosciences, San Diego, CA, USA) were coated with TGFβ1-LAP (5 μg/cm2) or BSA (0.1%) o/n at + 4 °C. The wells were blocked with 0.1% BSA (1 mg/ml, RT, 1 h). The wells were treated with PAD2, PAD4, or PAD4 supplemented with Cl-amidine (500 μM; N-α-benzoyl-N5-(2-chloro-1-iminoethyl)-l-Orn amide; Merck MilliPore) as described above. In the case of PAD4 and PAD4-Cl-amidine, a 45 min treatment was used. Detached HaCaT or MG-63 cells (trypsinized) were seeded to the wells (15 000 per well) in serum-free DMEM. Cell spreading was measured with the xCELLigence system (Roche Applied Science).
Protein binding assay
Plates (96-well DNA-BIND; Corning Costar, Corning, NY, USA) were coated overnight with active TGFβ1 (recombinant human, R&D Biosystems; diluted in PBS, 0.5 μg/cm2) or BSA (1 mg/ml) in + 4 °C. The wells were blocked with BSA (1 mg/ml, RT, 1 h). After citrullination by PAD2 (0.8 U/ml, o/n at + 37 °C), the wells were washed, and a recombinant TGFβRII-Fc chimera (human–mouse, R&D Biosystems; 30 ng/ml) was allowed to bind for 1 h at room temperature in Delfia assay buffer (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts). After washing the wells, Eu-labeled anti-mouse antibody (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts) was added. After 1 h incubation, the wells were washed and enhancement solution added. Signal was detected with time-resolved fluorescence spectrophotometry (Victor3 multilabel counter; PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts). The washing steps were always three times with PBS.
For assays testing integrin αVβ6 binding (recombinant human, R&D Biosystems) to TGFβ1-LAP, the experiments were performed as described above but the wells were coated with αVβ6 integrin (diluted in PBS, 1.0 μg/cm2), and the citrullination of TGFβ1-LAP was performed in solution (citrullination buffer, 3 U/ml PAD2, 90 μg/ml TGFβ1-LAP). Heat-inactivated PAD2 was used as a control. Bound TGFβ1-LAP was detected with Eu-labeled anti-6xHis antibody (PerkinElmer, Waltham, Massachusetts).
The dilution of antibodies was 1/1000.
Mass spectrometry and data analysis
The citrullination TGFβ1-LAP was performed in solution (citrullination buffer, PAD2 1.4 U/ml, TGFβ1-LAP 0.1 μg/ml; 30 min at + 37 °C). The samples were acetone precipitated, alkylated, and digested by trypsin, and analyzed with nanoflow HPLC system (Easy-nLC1000, Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled to the Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with a nano-electrospray ionization source. The detailed description of mass spectrometry can be read from supplementary method. Tandem mass spectra were searched with Proteome Discoverer software, version 1.4. (Thermo Fischer Scientific) using Mascot search engine (version 2.5, Matrix Science) against human Swiss-Prot entries (UniProtKB release 2015_08) allowing for 5 ppm peptide mass tolerance and a fragment mass tolerance of 0.015 Da. Carbamidomethyl (C) as a fixed modification, and citrullination (R), deamidation (N, Q) and oxidation (M) as dynamic modifications were included. Maximum of two missed cleavages were allowed. Decoy database search using reversed human Swiss-Prot sequences was performed and false discovery rate was calculated by the Proteome Discoverer software. The peptides that were identified when the false discovery rate was set to < 0.05 were accepted for further analysis. The spectra leading to identification of citrullinated peptide (excluding those with C-terminal citrulline) were manually inspected with Proteome Discoverer software. Only the citrullinations that were visible in the b or y fragment ion or in the subsequent b or y ion series were accepted. The citrullinations that could not be reliably distinguished from deamidations of N or Q were omitted. The mass spectrometry proteomics data have been deposited to the ProteomeXchange Consortium (Vizcaíno JA, Deutsch EW, Wang R, Csordas A, Reisinger F, Ríos D, Dianes JA, Sun. Z, Farrah T, Bandeira N, Binz PA, Xenarios I, Eisenacher M, Mayer G, Gatto L, Campos A, Chalkley RJ, Kraus HJ, Albar JP, Martinez-Bartolomé S, Apweiler R,Omenn GS, Martens L, Jones AR, Hermjakob H (2014). ProteomeXchange provides globally co-ordinated proteomics data submission and dissemination [52] via the PRIDE partner repository with the dataset identifier PXD003132 and 10.6019/PXD003132.
Structural analysis
All three-dimensional structures of proteins were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB; www.rcsb.org) [53], including the 2.5 Å resolution structure of αVβ6 integrin ectodomain with the bound RGD peptide from the TGF-β3 sequence (PDB ID; 4UM9) [25]. In order to compare the possible consequences of citrullination of arginines on the binding of TGF-β growth factors with TGF receptors, the following X-ray structures were analyzed and compared: The 2.15 Å resolution structure of TGF-β3 bound to TGF-β-RII (PDB ID: 1KTZ) [54]; the 3.0 Å resolution structure of TGF-β3 bound to the TGF-β-RI/TGF-β-RII complex (PDB ID: 2PJY) [29]; and the 3.0 Å resolution structure of TGF-β1 bound to the TGF-β-RI/TGF-β-RII complex (PDB ID: 3KFD) [28]. Structures were analyzed and figures prepared using Bodil [55].
