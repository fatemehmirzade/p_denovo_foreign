=== TITLE ===
Photodynamic treatment with hexyl-aminolevulinate mediates reversible thiol oxidation in core oxidative stress signaling proteins.

=== ABSTRACT ===
Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a highly selective two-step cancer treatment involving a photosensitizer and illumination with visible light in the presence of molecular oxygen. PDT is clinically approved worldwide for treating several premalignant conditions and cancer forms, especially endoscopically accessible tumors and dermatological malignancies. PDT-mediated cytotoxicity takes place via autophagy, apoptosis and necrosis, but the exact trigger mechanisms for various death-pathways are still unknown. PDT induces reactive oxygen species (ROS) through photochemical reactions. ROS can react with different macromolecules resulting in cellular damage, including oxidation of proteins. One of the known protein modifications is reversible oxidation of cysteine thiols (-SH), which in many cases constitute a redox switch to modulate protein activity and cellular signaling. Here we have examined the role of reversible oxidation of protein thiols as a potential mediator of cytotoxicity after hexylaminolevulinate-mediated photodynamic treatment (HAL-PDT) in the human epidermoid carcinoma cell line A431. Nearly 2300 proteins were found to be reversibly oxidized after HAL-PDT, of which 374 high-confidence proteins were further allocated to cellular compartments and functional networks. 115 of the high confidence proteins were associated with apoptosis and 257 have previously not been reported to be reversibly oxidized on cysteines. We find an enrichment of DNA damage checkpoint and oxidative stress response proteins. Many of these constitute potential signaling hubs in apoptosis, including ATM, p63, RSK1 p38, APE1/Ref-1 and three 14-3-3 family members. Our study represents the first comprehensive mapping of reversibly oxidized proteins subsequent to HAL-PDT. Several of the proteins constitute potentially novel redox-regulated apoptotic triggers as well as potential targets for adjuvants that may improve the efficacy of HAL-PDT and PDT using other photosensitizers.

=== METHODS ===
Chemicals High capacity neutravidin agarose and trypsin were from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, USA). Complete, EDTA free protease inhibitor was from Roche (Basel, Switzerland). Antibiotics and reagents for electrophoresis were from Life Technologies (Paisley, UK). Cell culture reagents were from BioWhittaker (Lonza, Belgium). Carboxy-H2DCFDA (DCF) was from Invitrogen (Lofer, Austria). Hexylaminolevulinate (HAL) was kindly provided by Photocure ASA (Oslo, Norway). All other chemicals were from Sigma Aldrich (Missouri, USA). Cell culture A431 (ATCC CRL-1555) epidermoid carcinoma cells were grown (dark conditions, 371C, 5% CO2, humidified atmosphere) in DMEM supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS, 2 mM L-glutamine, 100 U ml 1 penicillin, 0.1 mg ml 1 streptomycin, 2.5 mg ml 1 amphotericin B, 10 mM HEPES and 1 mM Na-pyruvate. Cells were seeded in appropriate cell culture dishes on the day before treatment. Cell numbers (total, live and dead) were determined using the Countesst system (Invitrogen, Paisley, UK) and the number of live cells used for calculating seeding. HAL-mediated photodynamic treatment 10 mM HAL dissolved in PBS (pH 6.0) was prepared on the day of the experiment and sterilized by filtration (0.2 mm pore size). Culture medium was replaced by serum-free medium containing 20 mM HAL for 3 hours and then by culture medium without HAL. Samples were then immediately illuminated from below using an in-house built LED array emitting at 624 nm with a fluence rate of 35.0 mW cm 2 and then placed in the incubator until further processing.24,25 All subsequent steps were performed under subdued light. Dose–response analyses One day prior to treatment, 104 A431 cells per well (100 ml) were seeded in 96-well microplates (Optilux black, Falcon, VWR, Norway), giving 50–60% confluence at the day of treatment. Wells at the periphery contained medium only to minimize evaporation. Samples in each microplate encompassed ‘controls’ (no HAL, no light), ‘light-only’ (no HAL, maximal light intensity), ‘HAL-only’ (20 mM HAL, kept in the dark) and ‘HAL-PDT’ samples. Resazurin assay 1 and 22 hours after illumination, 20 ml resazurin (2.5 mM) was added to each well and incubated for two hours in the dark. Resorufin fluorescence was determined using a BMG Labtech FluoStar Omega microplate reader (excitation 544 nm, emission 590 nm). After the fluorescence reading 3 hours post HAL-PDT the medium was replaced with 100 ml fresh medium and fluorescence reading was repeated 24 hours post HAL-PDT. Three independent biological replicate experiments were undertaken, each including 6 parallels. ROS measurement Intracellular ROS were measured indirectly by carboxy-H2DCFDA (DCF)-labelling and quantified by flow cytometry.26,27 5 105 A431 cells were seeded in 3.5 cm Petri dishes one day before HAL-PDT. For the last hour of incubation with HAL (20 mM, 3 h), DCF was added to a final concentration of 50 mM. Cells were illuminated (624 nm, 35 mW cm 2) for various time points, incubated further for 10 min, washed twice (PBS) and detached by Accutase (750 ml). Detached cells were centrifuged at 850 g for 3 min. Cell pellets were resuspended in PBS (400 ml) and transferred to FACS tubes prior to flow cytometry analysis (lex = 488 nm, lem,max = 525 nm) in a Becton Dickinson FACS Canto II (BD Austria, Schwechat, Austria) flow cytometer. Three indepen- dent biological replicate experiments were undertaken. Selective biotinylation of reversibly oxidized thiol proteins Reversibly oxidized thiol proteins were selectively biotinylated as described, with minor modifications.22,28,29 After HAL-PDT (20 mM, 3 h) with a fluence mediating 100% cell death (12.5 J cm 2 , based on dose–response experiments), cells were incubated for 30 min in normal culture medium. The cells were washed twice in ice cold PBS before addition of 1.5 ml lysis buffer (40 mM HEPES- NaOH pH 7.4, 200 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1 mM EGTA, 1% CHAPS, 100 mM N-ethylmaleimide (NEM) and protease inhibitors (complete, EDTA-free)) to each 15 cm dish (90% confluence) and kept on ice for 15 minutes. Cells were collected by scraping, further homogenized by pipetting and cleared by centrifugation (20 min, 16 000 g, 41C). Supernatants were snap-frozen in liquid N2 and stored at 80 1C. After thawing, excess NEM was removed by size-exclusion chromatography through Sephadex G-25 and fresh DTT was added to the pooled protein-containing fractions to a final concentration of 1 mM. Samples were incubated in room temperature for 10 min to reduce any oxidized thiols, biotin maleimide was added to a final concen- tration of 1.5 mM to block free SH-groups and the samples were incubated on a tilting board for one hour at room temperature. After additional size-exclusion chromatography to remove DTT and biotin maleimide, protein-containing fractions as deter- mined from A280 absorption were pooled and stored at 80 1C until analysis. The experimental workflow is illustrated in Fig. S1 (ESI†) and undertaken in three independent biological replicates for both controls (no HAL, no light) and HAL-PDT treated A431 cells. Aﬃnity enrichment of biotinylated proteins NeutrAvidin resin (binding capacity 8 mg protein per ml) was washed twice in PBS, and a volume of resin with capacity to bind 50% of the total protein was added to each selectively biotinylated sample and incubated for two hours at room temperature on a tilting board. Each sample was transferred to a column and subjected to a three-step wash with PBS, PBS with 0.5% Tween-20 and PBS. Excess PBS was removed by gravity flow, the column outlet sealed and a volume of elution buﬀer (1 NuPAGE LDS sample buﬀer, 100 mM DTT) equal to the resin volume was added. After sealing of the column inlet, samples were heated at 901C for 10 min. Eluted proteins were analyzed by electrophoresis on 4–12% bis–tris gels. Protein bands were visualized by Coomassie blue staining and each of the six lanes (three controls, three HAL-PDT) were cut into 20 slices for MS analysis in parallel with a blank gel slice as background control. In-gel digestion and LC-MS/MS analysis Gel slices (120 + background) were crushed, destained and subjected to in-gel trypsination as described.30 After desalting and drying, peptides were resuspended in 0.1% formic acid before LC-MS/MS analysis on an Easy-nLC 1000 UHPLC system (Thermo Scientific/Proxeon) interfaced with an QExactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) via a nanospray ESI ion source (Proxeon, Odense, Denmark). Peptides were injected onto an Acclaim PepMap100 C-18 trap column (75 mm i.d. 2 cm, C18, 5 mm, 100 Å, Thermo Scientific) and an Acclaim PepMap100 C-18 analytical column (75 mm i.d. 50 cm, C18, 3 mm, 100 Å, Thermo Scientific) using a 80 min gradient (250 nl min 1) from 0–30% CH3CN, 0.1% formic acid. Peptides were analyzed in positive ion mode under data dependent acquisition (DDA) using the following parameters: electrospray voltage 1.9 kV, HCD fragmentation with normalized collision energy 30, automatic gain control target value of 3 106 for Orbitrap MS and 1 105 for MS/MS scans. Each MS scan (m/z 400–1600) was acquired at a resolution of 70 000 FWHM, followed by 10 MS/MS scans triggered for intensities above 1.7 104, at a maximum ion injection time of 100 ms for MS and 60 ms for MS/MS scans. In total, 121 LC-MS/MS runs were performed. Mass spectrometry data analysis The 121 MS raw files from the experiments were analyzed using a Proteome Discoverer v2 using SequestHT.31 The search was performed against Human proteome (Uniprot November 2015 release) using trypsin cleavage and maximum two mis-cleavages. Precursor mass tolerance was 20 ppm and fragment mass tolerance 0.02 Da with Biotin:Sigma-B1267, N-ethylmaleimide, N-terminal acetylation, oxidation (M) and deamidation (NQ) as dynamic post-translational modifications based on Preview.32 Percolator33 was used for statistical validation of the identification with q-value cut-oﬀ 0.01. Identifications were further restricted to high confi- dence peptides, implying false discovery rate of o0.01. Proteins identified in less than two biological replicates after treatment or identified in the controls were filtered out using bash scripts and analyzed with IPAs. The results were parsed with PERL v 5.14.2 scripts to obtain subcellular locations by mapping to GO.34 Potential involvement in redox and apoptotic processes were analyzed by mapping to dbGSH,35 RedoxDB35,36 and cell death proteomics database.37,38 Since identifiers provided by diﬀerent databases vary across time and context, IPAs was used for cross- comparisons. This led to some shortening of the lists due to the presence of isoforms, synonymous names and obsolete entries or ID such as IPI. Nevertheless, this approach was more robust for our purpose.
