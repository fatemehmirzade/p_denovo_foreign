=== TITLE ===
Exome-based proteogenomics of HEK-293 human cell line: Coding genomic variants identified at the level of shotgun proteome

=== ABSTRACT ===
Genomic and proteomic data were integrated into the proteogenomic workflow to identify coding genomic variants of Human Embryonic Kidney 293 (HEK-293) cell line at the proteome level. Shotgun proteome data published by Geiger et al. (2012), Chick et al. (2015), and obtained in this work for HEK-293 were searched against the customized genomic database generated using exome data published by Lin et al. (2014). Overall, 112 unique variants were identified at the proteome level out of ∼1200 coding variants annotated in the exome. Seven identified variants were shared between all the three considered proteomic datasets, and 27 variants were found in any two datasets. Some of the found variants belonged to widely known genomic polymorphisms originated from the germline, while the others were more likely resulting from somatic mutations. At least, eight of the proteins bearing amino acid variants were annotated as cancer-related ones, including p53 tumor suppressor. In all the considered shotgun datasets, the variant peptides were at the ratio of 1:2.5 less likely being identified than the wild-type ones compared with the corresponding theoretical peptides. This can be explained by the presence of the so-called “passenger” mutations in the genes, which were never expressed in HEK-293 cells. All MS data have been deposited in the ProteomeXchange with the dataset identifier PXD002613 (http://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org/dataset/PXD002613).

=== METHODS ===
2.1 Cell culture

HEK-293 cells were obtained from ATCC (accession number CRL-1573; Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in DMEM containing 4.5 g/L glucose, 10% heat-inactivated FBS, 100 U/mL Penicillin–Streptomycin in humidified conditions under 5% CO2. Cells were collected by trypsinization and washed four times with Hank's Balanced Salt Solution. The substances for cell culturing and collecting were purchased from Gibco (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Cells from the 40th passage of HEK-293 were used for one-shot proteome analysis and for peptide fractionation. As a biological replicate, the 41st passage was used for an additional one-shot analysis.

2.2 Cell lysis, protein extraction, and in-solution trypsin digestion

Cell pellet containing 106 cells was resuspended in 100 μL lysis solution containing 0.1 % w/v Protease MAX Surfactant (Promega, USA), 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate, and 10% v/v ACN. The cell lysate was stirred for 60 min at 550 rpm at room temperature. The mixture was then subjected to sonication by Bandelin Sonopuls HD2070 ultrasonic homogenizer (Bandelin Electronic, Berlin, Germany) at 30% amplitude using short pulses for 5 min. The supernatant was collected after centrifugation at 15 700 × g for 10 min at 20°C (Centrifuge 5415R; Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany). Total protein concentration was measured using bicinchoninic acid assay (BCA Kit; Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

Two microliters of 500 mM DTT in 50 mM triethylammonium bicarbonate buffer were added to the samples to the final DTT concentration of 10 mM, followed by incubation for 20 min at 56°C. After that, 2 μL of 500 mM iodoacetamide (IAA) in 50 mM triethylammonium bicarbonate were added to the sample to the final IAA concentration of 10 mM. The mixture was incubated in the darkness at room temperature for 30 min.

The total resultant protein content (150–200 μg depending on the cell batch) was digested with trypsin (Trypsin Gold; Promega, Madison, WI, USA). The enzyme was added at the ratio of 1:40 w/w to the total protein content and the mixture was incubated overnight at 37°C. Enzymatic digestion was terminated by addition of acetic acid (5% w/v).

After the reaction was stopped, the sample was stirred (500 rpm) for 30 min at 45°C followed by centrifugation at 15 700 × g for 10 min at 20°C (Centrifuge 5415R; Eppendorf). The supernatant was then added to the filter unit (10 kDa; Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) and centrifuged at 13 400 × g for 20 min at 20°C in the same centrifuge. After that, 100 μL of 50% formic acid was added to the filter unit and the sample was centrifuged at 13 400 × g for 20 min at 20°C. Sample was dried up using a vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf) at 45°C. Dried peptides were stored at −80°C until the LC-MS/MS analysis or prefractionation.

2.3 Peptide prefractionation

RP chromatography prefractionation of the digests was performed using a Rainin Dynamax SD-200 system (Mettler Toledo, Columbus, OH, USA) and Phenomenex Gemini C18 column (Phenomenex Inc., Torrance, CA, USA), average pore size 110 Å, particle size 5 μm, length 250 mm, and internal column diameter 2 mm. The amount of peptides loaded on the column for prefractionation was about 150 μg. The peptides were eluted by nonlinear gradient using solvents A (99.9% water, 0.1% TFA) and B (99.9% ACN, 0.1% TFA).  The optimized gradient was as follows: from 4 to 7% buffer B in 1 min, then to 32% B in 25 min, and finally to 70% B in 5 min. The flow rate was 0.3 mL/min. In total, ten fractions were collected. The end times for the fractions were 1.2, 7.2, 10, 12.1, 14.1, 16.1, 18.1, 20.3, 24.2, and 30 min, respectively. The collection started from the flow-through peak, which corresponded to 0 min. After prefractionation, the samples were dried up using a vacuum concentrator (Eppendorf) at 45°C and stored at −80°C until the LC-MS/MS analysis.

2.4 Liquid chromatography of HEK-293 digested sample

The chromatographic separation of an unfractionated HEK-293 sample (one-shot analysis) was performed using Ultimate 3000 system equipped with RSLC Nano pump and an autosampler (Dionex, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). EASY-Sprayۛ PepMap RSLC C18 column (500 mm × 75 μm, 2 μm particle size, 100 Å pore size; Dionex) was used in combination with enrichment Acclaim C18 PepMapۚ100 microprecolumn (300 μm id × 5 mm, 5 μm particle size, 100 Å pore size; Dionex). A combination of mobile phase A, composed of 0.08% formic acid, 0.015% TFA in water, and mobile phase B, composed of 0.08% formic acid, 0.015% TFA in ACN, was used for analytical separation in the elution gradient at a flow rate of 0.3 μL/min. Mobile phase C, composed of 0.08% formic acid, 0.015% TFA in 2% ACN, was used for sample loading onto the enrichment microprecolumn at a flow rate of 8 μL/min. The gradient applied for peptide elution started from 2% of mobile phase B, had an increase to 5% over the first 19 min, then to 19% B over 133 min, and finally to 30% B over 28 min. The column was washed with 80% B for 12 min followed by subsequent equilibration of the system with 2.5% of mobile phase B for the next 25 min.

2.5 Liquid chromatography of the prefractionated HEK-293 digested sample

The fractions of HEK-293 sample (Section 2.3) were analyzed using the chromatographic system, column, and binary solvent composition described in the previous section. Each of the ten fractions was run in three technical replicates, about 4.5 μg of total peptide amount was loaded onto the column for each run as described in Section 2.4. The elution gradient started from 3% B for the first 3 min and then increased to 7 % B over the next 4 min, then to 35% B over 28 min, and the gradient was rapidly increased to 80% B in 5 min. Column washing was performed for 10 min with the follow-up rapid decrease in the content of mobile phase B down to 3% over 5 min, followed by the system equilibration under initial gradient conditions (3% of mobile phase B) for the next 30 min. A blank run was performed between the analyses of different fractions to reduce the carry-over effect. The total analysis time was 110 min with the gradient elution time of 65 min.

2.6 High-resolution MS of unfractionated and prefractionated HEK-293 samples

A high-resolution Q-Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for LC-MS/MS analysis of both unfractionated and fractionated HEK-293 samples. The analysis was performed in data-dependent acquisition mode using a top-20 method; the duration of the analysis was 240 min for the unfractionated samples and 110 min for each of the fractions of the C18-fractionated sample. Nanoflow Easy-Sprayۚ ion source was used in positive polarity mode for the analysis. The capillary temperature was 280°C, spray voltage was set at −1.9 kV and S-lens RF level was adjusted to 83%.  The MS spectra were acquired at resolving power of RFWHM = 70 K with target value of 3e6 ions or maximum accumulation time of 250 ms. The scan range for MS acquisition was set from 400 to 1200 m/z. Peptide ions were fragmented using high-energy collision dissociation with normalized energy set at 25% and stepped ramping ±20%. The underfill ratio was set at 0.1% with exclusion of singly charged ions. The MS/MS spectra were acquired at resolving power of RFWHM = 17.5 K and target value of 2e5 ions or maximum integration time of 120 ms. The first fixed mass was set at 150 m/z and the isolation window was 4 Th. Active dynamic exclusion mode was applied and an exclusion time was set at 15 s. Raw HPLC-MS/MS data for HEK-293 cell line were uploaded to the ProteomeXchange repository, project accession number PXD002613.

2.7 External data of HEK-293 lineage deep proteome

Raw data for six fractions of HEK-293 peptide digest analyzed earlier by LC-MS/MS using an Orbitrap FTMS were kindly provided by the authors 8. Raw data for the analyses of 24 peptide fractions of HEK-293 digest performed by Chick et al. 9 were downloaded from ProteomeXchange repository, accession number PXD001468.

2.8 Variant peptide database for proteome search generated from exome data

Polymorphisms and mutations found in the HEK-293 genome 10 were annotated by the SNPnexus software 13. The SNPnexus output was processed using in-house developed Python script to generate a protein sequence database, which was concatenated with human Uniprot database (version 02/2015). The database of tryptic variant peptides was generated using another in-house developed Python script based on the publicly available Pyteomics library 14. The generated variant peptide database contained peptides with the lengths from five to 70 amino acids that are neither present in the reference Uniprot human proteome database (version 02/2015), nor in the list of common contaminants.

2.9 Data processing with X!Tandem search engine

DeMix software 15 was used to generate the mgf files accounting for the mixed fragment mass spectra. Both peptide and protein identifications were obtained using X!Tandem search engine 16. The digest specificity was set to trypsin cleavage rule with maximum of two missed cleavages allowed. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues was considered as fixed modification, and oxidation of methionine and acetylation of the N-terminus of proteins were considered as variable modifications during the search. The precursor mass tolerance was set at 10 ppm, the fragment mass tolerance was set at 0.01 Da. The identifications were filtered to 1% FDR at the peptide spectrum match (PSM), peptide, and protein levels using MPscore software 17.

For the variant peptide identification based on the variant database described in Section 2.8, a group-specific FDR calculation method was used, which provided separate FDR for the variant peptides. All nonvariant PSMs were excluded from the analysis before the FDR filtering. A PSM was considered variant-related if all its protein candidates came from the variant database. Thus, the peptides from the nonmutated parts of mutant protein sequences had both variant and wild-type proteins in the candidate lists and were further excluded from the analysis. 2.10 Data processing using MaxQuant software

All raw files were processed using MaxQuant, version 1.5.3.12 20, which included Andromeda as database search engine. Most of the search parameters were set at the values used for the X!Tandem searches above. The MaxQuant analysis included an initial search with precursor mass tolerance of 100 ppm, the results of which were used for mass recalibration. Over the main Andromeda search, the precursor and the fragment mass tolerances were set at 10 ppm and 0.01 Da, respectively. The search included methionine oxidation and N-terminal acetylation as variable modifications, as well as cysteine carbamidomethylation as a fixed modification. The minimal peptide length was set to seven amino acids, and up to two missed cleavages were allowed. The FDR was set to 1% for both peptide and protein identifications. For the analysis of the prefractioned sample, the “match between runs” option of MaxQuant software was enabled.
